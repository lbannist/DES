# CAD 11 – Assemblies, Fasteners & Handoff Documentation

## Unit D: Assemblies, Fasteners & Handoff Documentation — Challenge D3

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (5–6 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Reverse-engineer a real, multi-part product by measurement alone, with no tutorial or reference drawing
- Model every part of a chosen product and assemble them correctly
- Generate a BOM and exploded view for a product they did not design themselves
- Work through the out-of-the-box thinking required when a product's geometry isn't obvious at a glance

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What does it take to fully understand a product well enough to rebuild it from nothing but the object itself?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Selection & Measurement (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Choose a Product and Take It Apart (Digitally)**

1. Choose a real product made of multiple parts (for example, a Plastic Screw-Tight Round Post Insulator) — something deceptively simple-looking but with real geometric complexity once you look closely.
2. Disassemble the product if possible, and measure every part using calipers — no tutorial, no reference drawing.
3. Sketch or note each part's key features and how they interact with the others (threads, snap-fits, internal clearances, etc.).

**Requirements**

- Chosen product documented with measurements for every part
- Notes identifying how the parts interact (threading, snap-fit, clearance, etc.)
- No tutorial or manufacturer drawing used at any point

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Modeling & Assembly (Days 3–5)

**✏️ Rebuild the Product in Fusion 360**

1. Model each part of the product individually, using only your own measurements.
2. Bring the parts together into an assembly, applying joints that reflect how the real product actually goes together.
3. Compare your modeled assembly against the real product — check that parts mate the way the original does, not just that they look similar.
4. Generate a complete BOM and exploded view from your assembly.

**Requirements**

- Every part of the product modeled individually from measurement
- Complete, correctly joined assembly matching the real product's behavior
- BOM generated listing every part
- Exploded view showing how the product goes together

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What part of this product looked simple but turned out to be more complex once you measured it closely?
2. Where did you have to make a judgment call because a feature was hard to measure precisely?
3. How did modeling this product change how you think about "simple" everyday objects?
4. What would you do differently if you reverse-engineered a different product next?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Measurement & Analysis (25%)**

- Accurate, complete measurements taken independently
- Clear identification of how parts interact

**Modeling & Assembly (35%)**

- Every part modeled correctly from measurement
- Assembly behavior matches the real product

**BOM & Exploded View (20%)**

- Complete, accurate BOM and exploded view

**Reflection (20%)**

- Honest account of unexpected complexity found
- Clear articulation of judgment calls made

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Even the simplest-looking product hides real design decisions — reverse-engineering it properly means noticing the complexity a quick glance would miss.
