# CAD 11 – Manufacturing-Ready Modeling

## Unit C: Manufacturing-Ready Modeling — Challenge C4

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (5–6 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Generate CAM toolpaths for a multi-part assembly, not just a single solid
- Fabricate real, physical parts by CNC-routing (or 3D printing, if the CNC is unavailable)
- Fit-check and assemble their own fabricated parts into a finished physical object
- Diagnose and correct a digital model based on how it behaves in the real world

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What does your model still have to prove once it's not just sitting on a screen anymore?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — CAM for Multiple Parts (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Generate Toolpaths for Every Part**

1. Using your C3 toy plane model, set up stock and WCS placement for each individual part.
2. Generate toolpaths appropriate to each part's geometry and orientation.
3. Simulate every toolpath and resolve any collisions or gouges before moving on.
4. If any part is not realistically CNC-routable at this scale or geometry, prepare it for 3D printing instead and note why.

**Requirements**

- Toolpaths (or print-ready files) generated for every part in the assembly
- Full simulation run for all CNC toolpaths, with no collisions or gouges
- A brief note on which fabrication method was used for each part and why

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Fabrication & Assembly (Days 3–5)

**✏️ Build the Real Thing**

1. CNC-cut (or 3D print) all parts of your toy plane.
2. Attempt to assemble the finished physical parts using the interlocking features designed in C3.
3. Identify any fit problems — parts that are too tight, too loose, don't align, or don't physically go together as modeled.
4. Revise your digital model to fix any fit problems, and re-fabricate and re-test the affected part(s) if time allows.

**Requirements**

- All parts fabricated (CNC-cut and/or 3D printed)
- A documented assembly attempt, including any fit problems found
- At least one model revision made in response to a real fit problem
- Final assembled toy plane, or a clear account of what prevented full assembly

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What fit or assembly problem came up that you didn't expect from the digital model alone?
2. What did you change in the model to fix it, and how do you know the fix worked?
3. If a part had to be 3D printed instead of CNC-cut, what made that part unsuitable for the router?
4. Looking back at your C3 model, what would you design differently if you were starting this project over?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**CAM Setup & Toolpaths (30%)**

- Toolpaths generated and simulated cleanly for every applicable part
- Sound fabrication-method decisions (CNC vs. 3D print)

**Fabrication & Fit (30%)**

- Parts successfully fabricated
- Genuine assembly attempt with fit problems documented

**Model Revision (20%)**

- At least one real, justified revision made in response to a physical fit problem

**Reflection (20%)**

- Honest account of what the physical build revealed that the digital model didn't
- Clear articulation of what would change next time

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A model isn't finished when it looks right on screen — it's finished when the physical parts it produces actually fit together, and getting there usually means going back and fixing what the real world exposed.
