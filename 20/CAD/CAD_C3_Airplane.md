# CAD 11 – Manufacturing-Ready Modeling

## Unit C: Manufacturing-Ready Modeling — Challenge C3

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (5–6 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Model a real-world object from reference photos alone, with no dimensioned drawing
- Simplify a complex form down to what's needed for a toy-scale, exterior-only model
- Break a single design into a set of discrete parts sized for later fabrication
- Practice the judgment calls involved in translating a photo into physical dimensions

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How do you turn "here's what it looks like" into "here's what I can actually build"?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Reference Study & Concept (Day 1)

**✏️ Study the Aircraft and Plan Your Simplification**

1. Review the dozen supplied reference photos of your assigned (or chosen) airplane.
2. Identify the aircraft's key proportions and defining shapes (fuselage length vs. wingspan, wing shape, tail configuration) — you will not have a dimensioned drawing, so these come from careful comparison across photos.
3. Decide what to leave out: this is an exterior-only toy version, with no cockpit interior or functional mechanisms.
4. Sketch a rough part breakdown — which sections of the plane will become separate physical parts (fuselage halves, wings, tail, etc.) so they can later be assembled.

**Requirements**

- A short list of the aircraft's key proportions, estimated from the reference photos
- A one-paragraph or sketched plan for what will be simplified or omitted
- A rough part breakdown sketch showing how the model will split into separate, assemblable parts

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Modeling the Toy Plane (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build the Simplified Exterior Model**

1. Model the aircraft's exterior form using your reference photos and estimated proportions — no dimensioned drawing exists for this part.
2. Keep the level of detail appropriate to a toy: clean, recognizable exterior shapes rather than technical accuracy.
3. Split the model into the separate parts identified in Checkpoint 1, sizing each one so it can realistically be CNC-cut or 3D printed and later assembled by hand.
4. Add any interlocking features (tabs, slots, dowel holes) needed to make assembly possible without glue-only joints.

**Requirements**

- Complete exterior-only model recognizable as the assigned aircraft
- Model split into a defined set of separate, assemblable parts
- Interlocking or joining features included where parts meet
- Overall size appropriate for a physical toy (not full scale)

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What proportions or dimensions were hardest to estimate from photos alone, and how did you arrive at your final numbers?
2. What did you decide to simplify or leave out, and why?
3. How did you decide where to split the model into separate parts?
4. What's one part of this process where you had to make a judgment call with no clear right answer?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Reference Interpretation (25%)**

- Reasonable, defensible estimation of proportions from photos alone
- Aircraft is clearly recognizable in the final model

**Solid Modeling (35%)**

- Clean exterior form appropriate to a toy-scale model
- Sound simplification decisions (no unnecessary interior detail)

**Part Breakdown & Assembly Design (20%)**

- Logical split into separate, fabricable parts
- Interlocking/joining features present and workable

**Reflection (20%)**

- Clear articulation of estimation and simplification decisions
- Honest account of judgment calls made

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A photo can tell you what something looks like, but turning that into a model you can actually build means making — and defending — a series of judgment calls a dimensioned drawing would have made for you.
