## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Run a static stress study and interpret von Mises stress and displacement results
- Understand how adding ribs, changing wall thickness, or repositioning material prevents failure under normal use
- Reverse-engineer a mechanical part by measurement alone, with no drawing or tutorial
- Apply stress analysis to a part they modeled themselves, not just a supplied example

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How do you know a design will actually survive being used, instead of just looking like it should?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Stress Analysis Fundamentals (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Learn Stress Analysis on the Xbox Controller Shell**

1. Using the Xbox controller shell model, set up a static stress study for a realistic load case (a drop impact, repeated button-press flex, or grip-squeeze force).
2. Run the study and read the two key results: von Mises stress (where is it red?) and displacement (does it deflect more than it should?).
3. Iterate the geometry at least twice — add a rib, change a wall thickness, or round a stress-concentrating sharp corner — and re-run the study after each change.
4. Compare your before/after results and note what changed.

**Requirements**

- A completed static stress study with a clearly justified, realistic load case
- At least two geometry iterations, each with a re-run stress study
- Documented before/after comparison of stress and displacement results

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Reverse-Engineering & First-Pass Analysis (Days 3–5)

**✏️ Apply the Same Process to a Real Mechanical Part**

1. Using calipers only — no drawing, no tutorial — measure a supplied mechanical part.
2. Model the part in Fusion 360 and produce a full detail drawing from your model.
3. Set up and run a first-pass static stress study for the part's realistic use case, applying what you learned in Checkpoint 1.

**Requirements**

- Complete solid model built from measurement alone
- Full detail drawing produced from the model
- First-pass stress analysis run with a realistic, justified load case

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did the stress study on the controller shell reveal that you wouldn't have predicted just by looking at the shape?
2. Which of your two design iterations made the biggest difference to the results, and why?
3. What load case did you choose for your reverse-engineered part, and how did you decide it was realistic?
4. What's one thing about "designing isn't done until it's validated" that's clicking for you now?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Stress Analysis Fundamentals (30%)**

- Correctly set up study with a realistic load case
- Two meaningful geometry iterations with documented before/after results

**Reverse-Engineering & Modeling (30%)**

- Accurate model built from measurement alone
- Complete, correct detail drawing

**Applied Stress Analysis (20%)**

- Sound, realistic load case applied to the reverse-engineered part

**Reflection (20%)**

- Clear articulation of what the stress results revealed
- Honest account of the reasoning behind design choices

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A stress result isn't a pass/fail checkbox — it's evidence that tells you exactly where and how to redesign, and that habit is what separates a finished design from one that just looks finished.
