## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Model two parts designed to thread together correctly
- Match thread pitch and clearance between mating parts so they actually screw together
- Understand why "looks threaded" and "threads correctly" are not the same thing
- Build the threading foundation needed for the fastener work in later Unit D challenges

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What has to be true about two separate models for them to actually screw together in real life?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Modeling the Two Parts (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Model a Threaded Screw-Cap Assembly**

1. Follow the provided tutorial to model a simple two-part screw-cap assembly (e.g., a jar with a threaded lid).
2. Pay close attention to how thread pitch, diameter, and clearance are set on each part so they match.
3. Model both parts as separate components, not as a single fused body.

**Requirements**

- Two separate solid models: a body and a matching threaded cap/lid
- Thread pitch and diameter matched between the two parts
- Appropriate clearance included so the parts can actually thread together

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Assembly & Verification (Day 3)

**✏️ Bring the Parts Together and Test the Fit**

1. Bring both components into an assembly and apply a joint that reflects how they actually go together (e.g., a screw or rigid joint set up to move along the thread).
2. Animate or manually drive the joint to confirm the cap threads on and off without interference.
3. If the fit binds or is too loose, adjust the clearance on one part and re-test.

**Requirements**

- Both parts combined into a single assembly file
- A joint set up that reflects the threaded motion
- Confirmed, working fit with no modeled interference

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What's the difference between a thread that looks right and a thread that actually functions in an assembly?
2. What clearance value did you end up using, and how did you decide on it?
3. What would happen to this assembly if the two parts were 3D printed instead of machined — would your clearance still work?
4. What's one thing about matching thread pitch that you didn't expect going in?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Thread Modeling (35%)**

- Correct, matched thread pitch and diameter between the two parts
- Clean, well-organized part models

**Assembly & Fit (35%)**

- Correct joint setup reflecting real threaded motion
- Verified, working fit with no interference

**Reflection (30%)**

- Clear articulation of how clearance affects real-world fit
- Honest account of any adjustments made

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A thread that looks correct on screen means nothing until two separate parts actually screw together — clearance, not appearance, is what makes an assembly real.
