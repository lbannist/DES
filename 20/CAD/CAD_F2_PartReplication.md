# CAD 11 – Validation & Reverse-Engineering Capstone

## Unit F: Validation & Reverse-Engineering Capstone — Challenge F2

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (5–6 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Validate a model against both dimensional accuracy and real physical fit within an assembly
- Confirm fasteners and mating parts actually work with a fabricated part, not just the isolated piece
- Make and justify a deliberate design improvement based on combined fit and stress evidence
- Re-run a stress analysis to confirm an improvement actually helped

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Does your part actually belong in the machine it came from — not just match its own measurements?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Fabrication & Fit Testing (Days 1–3)

**✏️ Print and Test Your F1 Part in Context**

1. 3D print your Challenge F1 reverse-engineered model.
2. Check dimensional accuracy against the real part using calipers.
3. Test physical fit within the assembly or machine the part actually belongs to: do the bolts pass through cleanly? Does the part correctly mate with its neighboring component in the car or machine it came from?
4. Document any fit problems found — a hole that's too tight, a mating surface that doesn't sit flush, a bolt that binds.

**Requirements**

- 3D-printed part with documented dimensional accuracy check
- Documented fit test against the real assembly (bolts, mating parts — not just the isolated part)
- Clear record of any fit problems found

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Design Improvement & Re-Validation (Days 4–5)

**✏️ Improve the Design and Prove It**

1. Based on both your fit-test results and your F1 stress results, design and justify at least one deliberate improvement (added rib, changed wall thickness, rounded a stress concentration, adjusted a hole tolerance).
2. Re-run your static stress study on the improved design and compare it to your F1 baseline.
3. If time allows, re-print the improved part and re-test its fit in the real assembly.

**Requirements**

- At least one deliberate, justified design improvement, tied to real fit and/or stress evidence
- Re-run stress study with a documented before/after comparison
- A brief written explanation connecting the improvement to what the fit test or stress data actually showed

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What fit problem showed up in the real assembly that you couldn't have predicted from the isolated part alone?
2. What specific improvement did you make, and what evidence (fit or stress) led you to it?
3. Did your re-run stress study confirm the improvement helped, or was the result more complicated than expected?
4. What's the difference between a part that "matches its own dimensions" and a part that actually belongs where it's supposed to go?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Fit Testing (30%)**

- Genuine, documented fit test against the real assembly (not just the isolated part)
- Fit problems clearly identified and recorded

**Design Improvement (30%)**

- Improvement is deliberate, justified, and tied to real evidence

**Re-Validation (20%)**

- Stress study correctly re-run and compared to the F1 baseline

**Reflection (20%)**

- Clear connection drawn between evidence and design decision
- Honest account of what the results actually showed

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A part can match every dimension on your drawing and still not belong in the machine — real validation means testing it against the bolts, the mating parts, and the assembly it was actually built for.
