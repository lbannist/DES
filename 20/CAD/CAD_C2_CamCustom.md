# CAD 11 – Manufacturing-Ready Modeling

## Unit C: Manufacturing-Ready Modeling — Challenge C2

**Time:** 1–1.5 Weeks (4 Class Periods)

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Model a machinable part from a dimensioned drawing with no walkthrough or tutorial
- Transfer the CAM skills built in Challenge C1 to a new, unfamiliar part
- Make independent decisions about stock setup, WCS placement, and toolpath order
- Build confidence that CAM is a repeatable process, not a one-off recipe tied to a single tutorial part

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If nobody walks you through it step by step, what do you actually know how to do?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Reading the Drawing & Modeling (Day 1)

**✏️ Model From a Dimensioned Drawing Alone**

1. Review the supplied dimensioned drawing of a small machinable part (bushing, standoff, or threaded post).
2. Identify which features you've already built in C1 (threads, chamfers) and which are new to this part.
3. Model the part using only the drawing — no tutorial or walkthrough is provided for this challenge.
4. Check your model's dimensions directly against the drawing before moving on.

**Requirements**

- Complete solid model matching every dimension on the supplied drawing
- Correct feature order in the timeline (the part should still make physical sense if built in that order)
- No reliance on a tutorial — this model is built from the drawing alone

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Independent CAM Setup (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Produce Your Own Toolpath, Start to Finish**

1. Decide on your own stock size and material, and justify the choice in one sentence.
2. Place the WCS and set up your own toolpath sequence — you decide the order based on what you learned in C1, not a prescribed list.
3. Set feeds and speeds appropriate to your chosen stock.
4. Simulate the full toolpath and resolve any collisions or gouges before finishing.
5. Post-process the toolpath to the router's controller.

**Requirements**

- Toolpath sequence chosen and justified by the student, not copied from C1 step-by-step
- Feeds and speeds appropriate to the chosen stock
- Clean simulation with no collisions or gouges
- Posted G-code file included in your submission

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What from Challenge C1 transferred directly to this part, and what did you have to figure out on your own?
2. Where did you get stuck, and how did you work through it without a tutorial to fall back on?
3. How did you decide on your toolpath order, and would you change it if you did this again?
4. What's one decision here you're not fully confident about, and why?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Solid Modeling (30%)**

- Model matches every dimension on the supplied drawing
- Feature order is sound and buildable

**CAM Setup & Toolpath (35%)**

- Sound, independently chosen WCS placement and stock setup
- Feeds/speeds appropriate and justified
- Clean simulation with no collisions or gouges

**Technical Execution (15%)**

- Successfully posted, machine-ready G-code

**Reflection (20%)**

- Honest account of what transferred from C1 and what didn't
- Clear articulation of independent decisions made

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

CAM skill isn't something you did once on the spark plug — it's something you can do again, on a part you've never seen before, without someone walking you through it.
