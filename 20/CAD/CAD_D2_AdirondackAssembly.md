# CAD 11 – Assemblies, Fasteners & Handoff Documentation

## Unit D: Assemblies, Fasteners & Handoff Documentation — Challenge D2

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (5–6 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Build a complete multi-part assembly with real fasteners, following a full drawing package
- Generate a bill of materials (BOM) and exploded view directly from an assembly
- Translate CAD documentation into build plans usable by someone with no CAD background
- Understand documentation as a genuine handoff, not just a formality

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If you handed your plans to someone who has never opened Fusion 360, could they actually build this?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Building the Assembly (Days 1–3)

**✏️ Model the Adirondack Chair**

1. Work through the provided Adirondack chair design course (13-video series), taking the full drawing package and turning it into a complete 3D model.
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLLm7Yjr9z_z35RcdwKDTzw3Kg541mzxl2
3. Pay close attention to how fasteners are modeled and placed at each joint.
4. Confirm every part in the assembly is a separate component, correctly joined, not fused into a single body.

**Requirements**

- Complete multi-part chair assembly matching the provided drawing package
- All fasteners modeled and placed at their correct locations
- Parts correctly joined (not fused) so the assembly reflects real construction

---

## Checkpoint 2 — BOM, Exploded View & Build Plans (Days 4–5)

**✏️ Produce a Handoff Package for Someone Outside CAD**

1. Generate a full bill of materials (BOM) directly from your assembly, including every part and fastener.
2. Create an exploded view that clearly shows how the chair goes together, with balloon callouts tying back to the BOM.
3. Package the BOM and exploded view into a set of Lego-style build plans — step-by-step, visual, and written so a woodshop student with no CAD background could follow them and physically build the chair.
4. Test your build plans on a classmate (or have someone unfamiliar with the project read through them) and note where they got confused.

**Requirements**

- Complete BOM generated from the assembly (every part and fastener listed)
- Exploded view with balloon callouts referencing the BOM
- A Lego-style build plan document, sequenced step by step
- Documented feedback from at least one outside reader/tester

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What part of your build plans confused your outside reader, and how did you fix it?
2. What's the difference between documentation that's technically complete and documentation that's actually usable?
3. Where did modeling the fasteners force you to think more carefully about how the chair is actually built?
4. If a woodshop student built this chair entirely from your plans with no other help, what's the part most likely to go wrong?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Assembly Modeling (25%)**

- Complete, correctly joined multi-part assembly with fasteners modeled

**BOM & Exploded View (25%)**

- Accurate, complete BOM
- Clear exploded view with correct balloon callouts

**Build Plans & Usability (30%)**

- Step-by-step plans genuinely usable by someone with no CAD background
- Evidence of real outside-reader testing and revision

**Reflection (20%)**

- Honest account of where documentation failed and how it was fixed
- Clear articulation of the handoff problem

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Documentation isn't finished when it's technically accurate — it's finished when someone who has never seen your model can pick it up and actually build the thing.
