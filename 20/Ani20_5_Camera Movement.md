# Animation 20 – Camera Movement

## Animation Production Techniques: Cinematic Storytelling

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🔗 Building on the Last Assignment

In **Environmental Effects and Lighting**, you built a scene with foreground, midground, and background layers, plus atmosphere and lighting to make it feel immersive.

This assignment picks up where that one left off: instead of a static, single-view scene, you'll move a camera through it. The layered depth you already know how to build becomes more convincing once the camera is moving — depth reads more clearly when things are actually being revealed, not just stacked. You can reuse a scene from the last assignment, or build a new one using the same layering approach.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Create purposeful camera movement
- Improve visual storytelling
- Use framing and composition effectively
- Create dynamic scenes without complex character animation
- Understand how camera movement affects audience attention

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How can a static drawing become an engaging animation?

Professional productions often create movement through the camera rather than through characters.

A well-designed camera move can create excitement while dramatically reducing production time.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Camera Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Find one clip for each of the following camera moves** (film, TV, or animation — a couple minutes of searching per type is enough). Use a site like YouTube and search terms like "panning shot example":

- Panning shot
- Zoom
- Tracking shot
- Reveal
- Push-in

**✏️ Answer the Observation Questions in a short written response, using your five clips as reference (a few sentences per question is enough).** Keep this — you'll build on it in Checkpoint 2.

1. What is the camera trying to show?
2. What information is revealed?
3. How does movement affect mood?
4. When does the camera speed up or slow down?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Scene Planning (Day 1)

**✏️ Choose a scene:**

- Reuse your scene from Environmental Effects and Lighting
- Abandoned building
- Fantasy landscape
- City street
- Space station
- Original approved environment

**✏️ Create a 1-page storyboard**

Sketch 3–4 panels showing what the camera sees at key points along its move (e.g. start, midpoint, end, plus a reveal moment if you have one). For each panel, note:

- What's in frame
- Camera speed at that point (slow, steady, speeding up, slowing down)
- Starting camera position and ending camera position, marked on your scene

This storyboard is your blueprint for Checkpoint 3 — it's what turns "the camera moves" into a specific, plannable shot.

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your camera-movement animation in Krita, using your storyboard from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.** Draw a background larger than your viewport, then animate the camera moving across it so the motion meets the requirements below.

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 10–15 second animation
- Minimum 2 camera movements
- Background larger than viewport
- Camera motion must feel intentional
- Minimal character animation allowed

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did the camera reveal?
2. How did camera movement improve storytelling?
3. What animation work was avoided through camera motion?
4. What would you improve next time?
