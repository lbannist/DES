# DES 2012 – Smear Frames and Impact Flashes

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)

By the end of this assignment students will:

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How can one frame make an action feel ten times faster?

Professional animators rarely add more drawings to make something feel fast.

Instead they use:

- Smear frames
- Impact flashes
- Exaggerated poses

These techniques increase energy while reducing workload.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Action Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Watch a cartoon example first**

Start with one clearly exaggerated cartoon hit (punch, kick, or explosion — teacher-provided or class-selected). This makes the smear and impact flash unmissable before moving to a subtler reference.

**📖 Find your reference clip**

Choose or find **one specific reference clip** of a punch, kick, sword swing, dash, or explosion to study closely. This same clip will be the basis for your planning sketches in Checkpoint 2, so pick something with a clear, fast action.

**Observation Questions**

Answer these about your chosen reference clip:

1. Which frame feels fastest?
2. Where is motion blurred or distorted?
3. When does the impact occur?
4. What makes the hit feel powerful?
5. Which of the 12 principles of animation are doing the most work here — Timing? Exaggeration? Solid Drawing?
6. Comparing your reference clip to the cartoon example: what did the cartoon exaggerate, and why did that exaggeration work?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1)

**✏️ Use the same reference clip you analyzed in Checkpoint 1.**

Choose which type of action it shows:

- Punch
- Kick
- Sword slash
- Energy blast
- Dash attack
- Original approved action

**Plan**

Create sketches for the following, based directly on your reference clip. Each one is a chance to apply a specific principle from the 12 principles of animation — note the tag next to each pose:

- **Start Pose** *(Anticipation — the wind-up that tells the audience the hit is coming)*
- **Smear Frame** *(Timing — a single stretched/blurred drawing standing in for several frames of fast motion)*
- **Impact Frame** *(Exaggeration — pushed past realistic proportions for a split second to sell the force)*
- **Recovery Pose** *(Slow In/Slow Out — the body settles out of the hit rather than snapping still)*

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**✏️ Requirements**

- 3–5 second animation
- Minimum 2 smear frames *(Timing)*
- At least 1 impact flash *(Exaggeration)*
- Clear action sequence, with a readable pose held on at least one frame during the smear *(Solid Drawing)*
- Before, during, and after poses

Optional:

- Camera shake
- Glow effect
- Dust effects

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Efficiency Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. How many smear frames were used?
2. How much animation was replaced by smears?
3. What made the action feel powerful?
4. Which frame is most important?
5. How could you make the animation faster without adding frames?
6. Which principle made the biggest difference to how powerful the hit felt, and why?
