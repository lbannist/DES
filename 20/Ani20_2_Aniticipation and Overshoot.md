# Animation 20 – Anticipation and Overshoot

## Animation Production Techniques: Selling Motion Through Preparation and Recovery

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Create convincing anticipation poses
- Demonstrate overshoot and settling motion
- Apply principles of weight and momentum
- Integrate secondary motion into an action sequence
- Improve motion readability through staging

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Why do professional animations feel more believable than real-life video?

The answer is often found in what happens before and after an action.

Strong animators emphasize:

- Preparation
- Action
- Recovery

These stages help the audience understand motion and feel the weight behind it.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Motion Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Watch a cartoon example first**

Start with one clearly exaggerated cartoon jump, throw, or swing (teacher-provided or class-selected). This makes anticipation and overshoot unmissable before moving to a subtler reference.

**📖 Find your reference clip**

Choose or find **one specific reference clip** (human or animal motion — a real jump, throw, landing, swing, or push) to study closely. This same clip will be the basis for your planning sketches in Checkpoint 2, so pick something with a clear, visible action.

**Observation Questions**

Answer these about your chosen reference clip:

1. What movement happens before the action?
2. How does the body prepare?
3. Does the character/body stop immediately?
4. What parts continue moving after the action?
5. How does the action communicate weight?
6. Which of the 12 principles of animation are doing the most work in this clip — Anticipation? Slow In/Slow Out? Arcs?
7. Comparing your reference clip to the cartoon example: what did the cartoon exaggerate, and why did that exaggeration work?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning the Action (Day 1)

**✏️ Use the same reference clip you analyzed in Checkpoint 1.**

Choose which type of action it shows:

- Jump
- Throw
- Kick
- Sword swing
- Heavy object lift
- Original approved action

**Planning Sketches**

Create the following poses, based directly on your reference clip. Each one is a chance to apply a specific principle from the 12 principles of animation — note the tag next to each pose:

- **Anticipation Pose** *(Anticipation — the prep movement that tells the audience what's about to happen)*
- **Action Pose** *(Arcs — the body's main motion should travel along a curved path, not a straight line)*
- **Overshoot Pose** *(Exaggeration — pushed slightly past the realistic extreme to sell the force behind the action)*
- **Final Settle Pose** *(Slow In/Slow Out — the motion eases to a stop rather than snapping still)*

---



## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**✏️ Requirements**

- 4–6 second animation
- Clear anticipation *(Anticipation)*
- Main action, with visible squash & stretch on impact or extension *(Squash & Stretch)*
- Visible overshoot
- Settle motion *(Slow In/Slow Out)*
- At least one secondary motion element that lags behind the main body motion *(Secondary Action)*

Examples:

- Hair
- Clothing
- Cape
- Antenna
- Tail
- Loose accessory

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Efficiency Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which pose was most important?
2. Which section required the most frames?
3. How did anticipation improve readability?
4. How did overshoot improve realism?
5. What would you simplify next time?
6. Which principle made the biggest difference to how convincing the action felt, and why?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Technical Animation (40%)**

- Clear anticipation
- Convincing overshoot
- Smooth timing
- Consistent construction
- Visible arc in the main action's motion path
- At least one clear instance of squash & stretch
- Secondary action visibly lags behind the main body motion

**Motion Quality (30%)**

- Weight is believable
- Action reads clearly
- Secondary motion enhances animation

**Visual Presentation (20%)**

- Strong posing
- Clear silhouettes
- Professional export quality

**Reflection (10%)**

- Understanding of principles
- Production observations

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Great animation is often decided before the action starts and after it ends.

Professional animators focus on transitions—not just movement.
