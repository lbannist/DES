## 💭 Production Question

### How do you make an audience want to see more?

Many successful movies, games, television shows, and online animations begin with a single compelling moment.

The audience does not need the entire story. They only need enough information to ask questions.

Examples:

- Who is that character?
- What is behind that door?
- Why is the city abandoned?
- What caused the explosion?
- What happens next?

This assignment focuses on creating a short animated sequence that acts as a "hook" for a larger story.

---

## Assignment Overview

Create a short animation that introduces a mystery, conflict, discovery, or dramatic moment.

The animation should feel like the opening scene of a larger project.

The audience should finish the animation wanting more information.

## Checkpoint 1 — Story Development (Day 1)

**📖 Watch a worked example first:** [Pixar's *Up* — opening scene ("Married Life")](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8mzsU2O8tuU)

This is a famous example of a hook, but it works a little differently than the four story types below — it's worth watching before you plan your own. In about four wordless minutes, it shows a couple meeting, marrying, and growing old together, ending with the wife's death and the husband alone with a broken promise. There's no mystery, no danger, no cliffhanger in the usual sense — the audience doesn't finish it wanting more *information*. They finish it wanting to know whether Carl will keep his promise to Ellie. Call this a fifth type: **Emotional Investment** — the hook isn't a question about facts, it's a question about a person.

As you watch, consider:

1. What does the audience learn about Carl and Ellie in just a few minutes?
2. Nothing here is a mystery or a cliffhanger — so what makes you want to keep watching?
3. How much of this is told through action and imagery rather than dialogue? (There's almost no dialogue at all.)
4. Could a version of this technique work in a 10–15 second animation, or does it need the full four minutes to land?

**✏️ Create an animation concept that leaves the audience wanting more.** Choose one of the following storytelling approaches:

| Story Type | Description | Example Ideas | Video Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | A character finds something unexpected. | Ancient artifact, hidden message, secret doorway, strange creature |[Opening Scene from Coco (2017)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zNCz4mQzfEI)|
| Danger | A threat or problem is revealed. | Monster silhouette, incoming storm, alarm activation, approaching enemy | [Opening Scence from How to Train a Dragon (2010)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ATE5eT0c2AI)|
| Mystery | The audience receives incomplete information. | Unexplained object, abandoned location, strange signal, missing character | [Opening Scene from Wall-E (2008)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oMmyb04oXGY)|
| Cliffhanger | The animation ends just before a major event occurs. | Door opens, rocket launches, villain appears, artifact activates | [Ending Scene from the Incredibles(2004)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97wFcfjlrE0)|
| Emotional Investment | The audience cares about a character enough to want to know what happens to them. | A promise made, a goal stated, a relationship established, a loss suffered |[Opening Scene from Up (2009)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8mzsU2O8tuU)|

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your animation in Krita, using your Story Summary and Asset Plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.** Aim for one strong moment, not a complete story.

**Requirements**

Length:

- 10–15 seconds

Must include:

- One clear story moment
- One camera movement
- One previously learned animation technique — choose at least one:

  - Walk cycle
  - Anticipation and overshoot
  - Smear frame
  - Impact flash
  - Environmental effect
  - Lighting effect
  - Parallax
  - Tweening

**Production Limits**

Maximum:

- 3 major actions
- 2 camera movements

Students should focus on creating a strong moment rather than a complete story. The goal is not to explain everything — the goal is to create curiosity.

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Audience Test (Day 5)

**✏️ Show your animation to at least two classmates.** Ask them:

1. What do you think is happening?
2. What questions do you have?
3. What would you want to see next?

Record their responses.

## Checkpoint 5 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

2. What information did you intentionally hide?
3. Which animation techniques were used?
4. What assets were reused?
5. How did you reduce unnecessary work?
6. Did your audience want to know what happened next?
