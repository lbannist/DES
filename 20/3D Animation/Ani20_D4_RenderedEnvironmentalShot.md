## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Combine intermediate environment building, lighting theory, and render settings into one finished shot

- Optionally incorporate a character from Unit 1 into a finished environment

This is the unit capstone — every skill from Challenges D1–D3 should be visible here.

## 💭 Production Question

### What does it take for a still image to feel like a real, believable place?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Scene Finalization (Day 1)

**✏️ Finalize Your Environment**

1. Open your Challenge D1 environment and make any final modeling adjustments needed for a polished final shot.
2. Optionally, append your Challenge C1–C5 character into the scene and position it believably within the environment (check scale and ground contact).
3. Choose a final camera angle, applying the composition principles you've used in earlier units (subject placement, foreground/midground/background).

**Requirements**

- Environment finalized and ready for final lighting/render pass
- Character (if included) scaled and positioned believably

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Final Lighting Pass (Day 2)

**✏️ Apply Your Lighting Knowledge**

1. Set up three-point lighting and/or HDRI lighting (from Challenge D2) to establish your chosen final mood.
2. Adjust color temperature and light strength to fully commit to that mood.
3. Render test images and check for any technical issues (blown highlights, unreadable shadows) before finalizing.

**Requirements**

- Clear, intentional mood established through lighting
- No unresolved technical lighting issues in the test renders

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Final Render Settings & Output (Day 3)

**✏️ Choose and Justify Your Render Settings**

1. Decide between Eevee and Cycles for your final render, based on your Challenge D3 comparison test and this project's specific needs (time available vs. desired quality).
2. Set your sample count based on your Challenge D3 findings, balancing quality and render time for your actual deadline.
3. Set up your output format (image sequence recommended for safety) and render your final shot at full quality.
4. If rendering an animated camera move rather than a still, double check your Challenge D3 output-format reasoning applies here too.

**Requirements**

- Render engine choice is deliberate and justified, not arbitrary
- Sample count is reasonable for the project's timeline
- Final output is clean, high-quality, and technically correct

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Peer Critique (Day 3–4)

**✏️ Peer Critique**

1. Share your final rendered shot with a peer group without additional context.
2. Ask what mood or story they get from the image, and whether anything feels unfinished or unclear.
3. Note at least two specific pieces of feedback and how you'll address them before final submission.

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Reflection (Day 4)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which unit skill (environment complexity, lighting theory, or render settings) do you think had the biggest impact on your final shot?
2. Why did you choose the render engine and sample count you used for this piece?
3. If you included a character, how did it change the final composition and mood?
4. Looking back at this whole unit, what's one thing you want to carry forward into Grade 12's more advanced Unreal-based environment work?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Environment Quality (20%)**

- Environment shows clear complexity and craft from Challenge D1's techniques
- Scene reads as a cohesive, believable space
- Character (if included) integrated convincingly

**Lighting & Mood (30%)**

- Clear, deliberate mood established through lighting
- Color temperature and light strength choices support that mood consistently
- No unresolved technical lighting issues in the final render

**Render Quality & Technical Choices (25%)**

- Render engine choice is justified and appropriate to the project's needs
- Sample count balances quality and render time sensibly
- Final output is clean, high-resolution, and free of rendering artifacts
- Output format choice (sequence vs. video) is appropriate and considered

**Critique Response (5%)**

- Evidence peer feedback was genuinely considered and applied
- At least two specific pieces of feedback documented
- Clear explanation of what was changed as a result
- Feedback addressed before final submission

**Reflection (20%)**

- Thoughtful, specific reflection on the whole unit
- Clear identification of which skill had the biggest final impact
- Honest, specific justification for render engine/settings choices
- Concrete goal identified for Grade 12 environment work
