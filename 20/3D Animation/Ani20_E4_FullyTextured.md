## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Apply multiple material slots to a single object with distinct parts (e.g., skin vs. clothing, or metal vs. plastic on a prop)
- Combine node-based shading, PBR values, and procedural/image/painted texturing into one finished, cohesive material set
- Present a fully textured, render-ready model

This is the unit capstone — every skill from Challenges E1–E3 should be visible here.

## 💭 Production Question

### What does it take for a textured surface to feel like one believable object, made of several different materials?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Material Slot Planning (Day 1)

**✏️ Plan Your Materials**

1. Choose your final object: your Challenge C1 character (with clothing/accessories) or a hard-surface prop with multiple distinct parts.
2. In Edit Mode, identify and select the different regions that need different materials (e.g., skin, clothing, metal buckle, leather strap).
3. In the Material Properties tab, add a new material slot for each distinct region and assign the corresponding selected faces to each slot (select faces, select the material slot, click Assign).
4. Name each material slot clearly (e.g., "Skin", "Clothing_Fabric", "Metal_Buckle").

**Requirements**

- All distinct regions identified and assigned to separate, clearly named material slots
- Face assignment checked for accuracy (no leftover default-material faces where they shouldn't be)

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Building Each Material (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Build Out Each Material**

1. For each material slot, decide which approach(es) from Challenges E1–E3 fit best — a hand-painted texture for skin detail, a procedural noise for fabric weave variation, an image texture for a printed logo, etc.
2. Set appropriate Roughness/Metallic/Base Color values for each material based on what it's meant to represent.
3. Add Normal maps where appropriate for extra surface detail (fabric weave, scratched metal, skin pores).
4. Use Node Groups from Challenge E1 where they can save time across multiple similar materials (e.g., a reusable dirt/grime group applied with different settings to several materials).

**Requirements**

- At least three distinct materials fully built out (color, roughness/metallic, and texture as appropriate)
- At least two different texturing approaches used across the whole object (procedural, image-based, or painted)
- Materials are visually distinct and appropriate to what they represent

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Final Render & Polish (Day 3–4)

**✏️ Final Presentation Render**

1. Set up clean, neutral lighting (using your Unit 2 lighting knowledge) that shows off the materials clearly without overpowering them with mood/atmosphere.
2. Choose a render engine and sample settings (using your Challenge D3 reasoning) appropriate for a clean final presentation image.
3. Render at least two angles or a simple turntable so all major materials are visible.
4. Check the final render closely for any material that looks flat, unconvincing, or inconsistent with the others, and make final adjustments.

**Requirements**

- Clean, well-lit final render(s) showing all major materials clearly
- No obviously unconvincing or inconsistent materials remaining
- At least two viewing angles provided

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which material was hardest to get looking convincing, and what finally worked?
2. Which texturing approach (procedural, image-based, painted) did you rely on most for this object, and why?
3. How did planning your material slots in Checkpoint 1 save you time later in the process?
4. Looking at your finished object as a whole, does it read as one believable thing made of different materials, or as separate pieces? Why?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Material Planning & Assignment (20%)**

- All distinct regions correctly identified and assigned to separate material slots
- Material slots clearly named and organized
- Face assignment is accurate with no stray default-material areas
- Planning shows clear thinking about which parts need distinct treatment

**Material Quality (35%)**

- At least three fully built, visually convincing materials
- Roughness/Metallic/Base Color values appropriate to what each material represents
- Normal maps used where they add genuine value
- Node Groups reused sensibly where appropriate

**Texturing Variety (20%)**

- At least two different texturing approaches (procedural, image-based, painted) used across the object
- Each approach used where it's genuinely the best tool for that specific material
- Combination of approaches feels intentional, not arbitrary

**Final Presentation (10%)**

- Clean, well-lit final render(s)
- At least two angles provided showing all major materials
- No unresolved technical issues in the final render

**Reflection (15%)**

- Thoughtful, specific reflection on the whole unit
- Honest account of the hardest material to solve
- Clear reasoning for which texturing approach was relied on most
- Specific observation about whether the final object reads as one cohesive thing
