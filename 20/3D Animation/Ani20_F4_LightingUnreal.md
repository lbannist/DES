## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Import multiple Blender assets into a single Unreal scene
- Set up basic Unreal lighting (Directional Light and Sky Light) using Lumen
- Arrange props into a simple, intentional scene layout
- Apply the full Grade 11 pipeline: model/texture in Blender → simulate where relevant → import and light in Unreal

This assignment sets up the environment you'll turn into a full cinematic trailer in Challenge F5.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What does it take for a scene built across two different pieces of software to feel like one finished place?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Multi-Asset Import (Day 1)

**✏️ Bring In Your Assets**

1. Choose 3–5 assets from your Blender work this year (environment pieces from Unit 2, textured objects from Unit 3, or a physics-simulated detail baked to a static mesh from Challenge F1/F2) and export each following your Challenge F3 process.
2. Import all assets into a single Unreal project, using your Challenge F3 folder structure to keep things organized.
3. Check and correct scale on each asset as needed, applying what you learned in Challenge F3.
4. Arrange the assets into a simple scene layout — this doesn't need to be large, just intentional. Think ahead to Challenge F5: a scene with some sense of atmosphere or story potential will give you more to work with for a trailer.

**Requirements**

- At least 3 different assets successfully imported into one scene
- Scale corrected and consistent across all assets
- Assets arranged with a clear, intentional layout rather than randomly placed

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Lighting the Scene (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Set Up Unreal Lighting**

1. Add a Directional Light to represent your main light source (like the sun) and rotate it to establish a clear lighting direction.
2. Add a Sky Light to provide soft, ambient fill lighting from the environment.
3. Adjust light intensity and color on both to establish a specific mood (drawing on your Unit 2 lighting knowledge).
4. If Lumen is enabled in your project, observe how light bounces and interacts with your scene in real time as you adjust settings — note anything that surprises you compared to Blender's lighting behavior.

**Requirements**

- Both a Directional Light and Sky Light present and adjusted with intent
- A clear, identifiable mood established through lighting
- At least one observation made about how Lumen's real-time behavior compares to Blender

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Scene Polish (Day 3)

**✏️ Final Layout and Lighting Polish**

1. Set up a camera (or use the default viewport camera) to frame your scene using composition principles from earlier units.
2. Walk through the scene and check for any obvious problems — floating objects, harsh unintended shadows, assets clipping through each other.
3. Make final adjustments to asset placement and lighting until the scene feels cohesive.
4. Take a final screenshot or short flythrough capture of the finished scene.

**Requirements**

- Scene framed with intentional composition
- No unresolved technical issues (floating objects, clipping, obviously broken lighting)
- Final capture clearly shows the finished, polished scene

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 3–4)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which asset was trickiest to get working correctly in Unreal, and why?
2. How did lighting in Unreal feel different from lighting the same kind of scene in Blender?
3. Does this scene have a sense of mood or story potential you could build on for Challenge F5's trailer?
4. What's one thing about the Blender-to-Unreal pipeline you still want to get more comfortable with?

## 💡 Key Lesson

This is the first time in the program that a full pipeline spans two pieces of software from start to finish — model and texture in Blender, simulate where needed, then import and light in Unreal. In Challenge F5, this same scene becomes the foundation for something more ambitious: an actual cinematic trailer.
