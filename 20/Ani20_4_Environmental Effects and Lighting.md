# Animation 20 – Environmental Effects and Lighting

## Animation Production Techniques: Building Atmosphere

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Create animated environmental effects
- Use lighting and glow effects effectively
- Build mood and atmosphere
- Apply layered scene construction
- Create visual interest without character animation

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Can a scene feel alive without a character?

Professional productions often rely on subtle environmental movement to create immersion.

Small details can dramatically increase production value:

- Wind
- Rain
- Snow
- Fire
- Fog
- Lighting changes

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Scene Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Watch 2–3 examples of animated environments.**

Examples:

- Storm scenes
- Forest environments
- City streets
- Science fiction interiors
- Fantasy settings

**✏️ Answer the Observation Questions in a short written response (a few sentences per question is enough).** Keep this — you'll build on it in Checkpoint 2.

1. What is moving?
2. What creates mood?
3. How is lighting used?
4. Which effects repeat as loops? *(Timing — a well-made loop should read as continuous, natural motion, not a visible repeat)*
5. Which details add the most atmosphere?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Scene Planning (Day 1)

**✏️ Choose an environment:**

- Storm
- Snowfall
- Campfire
- Neon Alley
- Magical Forest
- Space Station
- Original approved environment

**📖 Find 1–2 reference photos**

Find real photography (not other animation or concept art) of your chosen environment. Staying grounded in real photos keeps your lighting and atmosphere choices believable.

**Write a short description and story**

- **Description (2–3 sentences):** What is literally in the photo? Time of day, weather, light source, what's moving.
- **The story:** What happened here, what's about to happen, or who might have just been present — even though no character will appear on screen.
- **The emotional target:** In one word or short phrase, what should this scene make the viewer *feel*? (Calm, tense, lonely, hopeful, threatening, awe...) This becomes the target your lighting and effect choices are aiming for.

**Plan**

Sketch the following, based on your reference photo and driven by your emotional target. This is an exercise in **Staging** — using composition and depth to lead the eye and establish mood before anything even moves:

- Foreground
- Midground
- Background
- Light source
- Environmental effects

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your full animated scene in Krita, using the layer plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.** Set up your document, then animate the environmental effects, lighting, and secondary motion so the scene meets the requirements below.

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 8–12 second animation
- Minimum 3 environmental effects, looping smoothly *(Timing)*
- Minimum 1 animated glow effect
- Layered depth *(Staging)*
- At least 1 secondary motion element *(Secondary Action)*

Examples:

Environmental Effects:

- Rain
- Snow
- Fog
- Fire
- Dust
- Falling leaves

Secondary Motion:

- Grass
- Cloth
- Trees
- Hanging cables

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Efficiency Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which effect added the most atmosphere?
2. Which effect required the least work?
3. How did lighting affect mood?
4. What loops were reused?
5. How could you improve the scene efficiently?
6. Does your final scene hit the emotional target you set in Checkpoint 2? What would you change to get closer to it?

## 💡 Key Lesson

A scene does not need a character to feel alive.

The most efficient animators use environment, lighting, and atmosphere to create motion without drawing every frame.
