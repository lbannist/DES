# Architectural Design 11 – Elevations & Building Section

## Unit D: Custom Home Design & Documentation — Challenge D2

**Time:** 1 Week (3 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Generate all four exterior elevations with correct annotation and dimensioning
- Cut and document at least one building section showing floor-to-floor heights and structure
- Apply consistent line weights, material call-outs, and grade lines across a drawing set
- Read a building in section, not just in plan

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What does a section show you that a floor plan never can?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Elevations (Day 1)

**✏️ Set Up and Annotate Four Exterior Elevations**

1. Open your custom home from Challenge D1 and create (or open) North, South, East, and West elevation views.
2. Check that materials, windows, and doors display correctly in each elevation — adjust visibility settings if anything is missing or showing incorrectly.
3. Add grade lines to each elevation showing where the building meets the ground.
4. Apply consistent line weights so structure, materials, and background elements are visually distinct.
5. Add material call-out tags to at least the primary exterior materials on each elevation.

**Requirements**

- All four elevations set up and correctly displaying the model
- Grade lines shown on each elevation
- Consistent line weights applied
- Material call-outs on primary exterior materials

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Building Section (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Cut and Document a Building Section**

1. Cut a section through a key space in your home — a stair, a double-height space, or wherever the most is happening vertically.
2. Check that the section correctly shows floor-to-floor heights, footing depth, and framing/structure.
3. Annotate floor-to-floor and overall building heights with dimension strings.
4. Add material or assembly call-outs where relevant (roof assembly, floor assembly, foundation).
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_D2_Elevations_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- A section cut through a meaningful part of the home
- Floor-to-floor and overall heights dimensioned
- Structure and key assemblies visible and legible
- At least one material or assembly call-out added

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did the section reveal about your design that you hadn't fully considered in plan view?
2. Which elevation was hardest to annotate cleanly, and why?
3. Why do grade lines matter on an elevation drawing?
4. How do line weights help a reader understand a drawing faster?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Elevations (35%)**

- All four elevations complete, correctly displayed, and annotated
- Grade lines and material call-outs present

**Building Section (35%)**

- Section cut through a meaningful location
- Heights dimensioned correctly, structure legible

**Technical Execution (10%)**

- Consistent line weights and drawing standards throughout

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific insight gained from working in section
- Clear articulation of drawing standard choices

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Plans tell you where things are; sections tell you how tall they are and what's holding them up — a design isn't fully resolved until you've checked it in both.
