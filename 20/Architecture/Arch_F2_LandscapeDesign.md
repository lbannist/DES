# Architectural Design 11 – Landscape Design

## Unit F: Neighbourhood Site Design & Rendering — Challenge F2

**Time:** 1 Week (2–3 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Grade the combined site around three buildings
- Add planting, hardscape, and street furnishing that connect the buildings, walkways, and street
- Design a landscape that ties separate lots together into one coherent neighbourhood scene
- Use Revit's site and planting tools to finish a civil-scale site

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What turns three buildings on lots into a neighbourhood instead of three unrelated sites?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Grading & Hardscape (Day 1)

**✏️ Shape the Ground and Add Walkways**

1. Adjust or refine the topo surface around each building so it reads as graded, not flat and untouched.
2. Add hardscape elements connecting each building to the street: front walkways, a driveway for the residential lots, and an entry path for the office.
3. Add any patios, decks, or other hardscape called for by each building's design.
4. Check that hardscape elements align sensibly with each building's entries.

**Requirements**

- Site grading refined around each building
- Walkways/driveways connecting each building to the street
- Hardscape aligned with building entries

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planting & Street Furnishing (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Add Planting and Furnish the Street**

1. Add planting beds and trees appropriate to each lot (front yard planting for the homes, entry planting for the office).
2. Add street furnishing connecting the whole site: benches, streetlights, signage, or similar elements along the sidewalk.
3. Vary planting types and placement so the site doesn't read as repetitive or copy-pasted between lots.
4. Review the site in a 3D or top-down view to confirm the landscape reads as one connected scene rather than three separate yards.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_F2_Landscape_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Planting added to all three lots with some variation between them
- Street furnishing added connecting the buildings to the street
- Site reviewed and reads as one connected landscape

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What specific choice (a planting type, a furnishing element, a path alignment) helped tie the three lots together?
2. Where did the landscape need adjustment after you saw it in 3D that you hadn't planned for at grading stage?
3. Why might a real neighbourhood's street furnishing and planting be planned as one system rather than lot by lot?
4. What's one landscape decision you'd revisit if you had another week on this site?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Grading & Hardscape (25%)**

- Site grading reads as intentional
- Walkways/driveways correctly connect buildings to the street

**Planting & Furnishing (40%)**

- Planting varied and appropriate to each lot
- Street furnishing ties the site together

**Cohesion (15%)**

- Site reads as one connected neighbourhood, not three separate yards

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific example of a design choice that improved cohesion
- Thoughtful account of a post-3D-review adjustment

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Landscape is what makes separate buildings feel like they belong to the same place — the street furnishing, the planting rhythm, and the connecting paths are doing as much work as the buildings themselves.
