# Architectural Design 11 – Furnishing & Construction Documents

## Unit E: Commercial Space Planning (Office Build) — Challenge E3

**Time:** 1–1.5 Weeks (4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Populate an office space with furniture families, workstations, and a basic lighting layout
- Produce a reflected ceiling plan (RCP) coordinated with the lighting layout
- Produce a finish schedule identifying materials and fixtures room by room
- Finish a commercial space the way it would actually be documented for construction

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What's still missing from a floor plan once the walls and doors are done?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Furnishing the Space (Day 1)

**✏️ Populate with Furniture and Workstations**

1. Place desk and seating families in the open workstation area, spacing them for reasonable circulation.
2. Furnish the private offices, meeting room, and reception with appropriate furniture families.
3. Lay out a basic ceiling lighting plan for the space, balancing ambient (general) and task lighting.
4. Check the furnished space in 3D to confirm nothing overlaps or blocks circulation.

**Requirements**

- Workstations and furniture placed throughout the office
- A basic ceiling lighting layout in place
- No overlapping furniture or blocked circulation paths

---

## Checkpoint 2 — RCP & Finish Schedule (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Document the Finished Space**

1. Set up a Reflected Ceiling Plan (RCP) view showing your lighting layout, ceiling type, and any ceiling-mounted elements.
2. Build a finish schedule listing flooring, wall finish, and ceiling finish for each room.
3. Cross-check that the finish schedule matches what's actually modeled in each room.
4. Produce a simple, clean floor plan showing furniture and workstations for the final set.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_E3_OfficeFinal_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- RCP correctly showing lighting and ceiling elements
- Finish schedule complete and matching the modeled materials
- A furnished floor plan ready for the final set

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did furnishing the space reveal about your original layout (things that felt tight, awkward, or that actually worked well)?
2. Why does a reflected ceiling plan need its own drawing instead of just being shown on the floor plan?
3. What's the purpose of a finish schedule, and who on a real project would actually use it?
4. What's one thing you'd change about your office layout now that it's fully furnished and documented?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Furnishing (25%)**

- Furniture and workstations placed appropriately with reasonable circulation

**RCP & Lighting (30%)**

- RCP correctly documents lighting and ceiling elements

**Finish Schedule (25%)**

- Schedule complete and matches the modeled space

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific, honest evaluation of the finished layout
- Clear understanding of who uses these documents and why

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Walls and doors make a space usable; furniture, lighting, and finish documentation make it buildable and livable — the second half of the work is what turns a floor plan into a real project.
