## Checkpoint 1 — Roof Redesign (Day 1)

**✏️ Change the Villa's Roof Form**

1. Open your Island Villa file from Challenge C1 and isolate the roof in a 3D view.
2. Choose one change to make: add a dormer, change one or more roof plane pitches, or introduce a hip-and-valley condition not present in the tutorial.
3. Edit the roof sketch (Edit Footprint or Edit Profile) to make the change, adjusting slope arrows and defining new roof planes as needed.
4. Check the result in a 3D view and in section — look for gaps, overlaps, or roof planes that don't actually meet.
5. Adjust wall heights or add roof-to-wall attachments where the new roof form meets the walls below.

**Requirements**

- At least one clear roof modification beyond the base tutorial (dormer, pitch change, or hip/valley)
- Roof planes correctly join with no visible gaps or overlaps
- Walls properly attached to the new roof form

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Envelope Check (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Verify the Building Envelope Still Works**

1. Check interior headroom in any space affected by the roof change (a bedroom under a new dormer, a hallway under a changed pitch) using a section cut.
2. Adjust ceiling heights, soffits, or roof geometry if headroom is now unreasonable.
3. Add basic roof detailing appropriate to the change — a dormer wall and window, fascia, or a flashing line — so the new form doesn't read as a floating shape.
4. Render a quick 3D preview of the updated exterior to confirm the roof reads clearly from outside.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_C2_RoofChallenge_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Interior headroom checked and reasonable under the modified roof
- Basic detailing added at the roof change (dormer wall/window, fascia, or flashing)
- A 3D preview showing the modified roof clearly

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What problem did Revit's automatic roof tool NOT solve for your change, and how did you solve it manually?
2. How did your roof change affect the interior space below it?
3. What would you do differently if you were adding an even more complex roof form?
4. Where else in a house design might a "simple on paper, complex in the model" detail like this show up?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Roof Modification (35%)**

- Clear, deliberate change to the roof form beyond the tutorial
- Roof planes correctly resolved with no gaps or overlaps

**Envelope & Detailing (30%)**

- Headroom checked and reasonable in affected spaces
- Basic detailing added at the point of change

**Technical Execution (15%)**

- Walls properly attached to the new roof
- Model is clean when viewed in 3D and section

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific account of what had to be solved manually
- Clear understanding of how the roof change affected the interior
- Thoughtful idea for a more complex future application

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Software tools generate a plausible starting point, not a finished answer — the roof-by-footprint tool gets you 80% of the way, and the last 20% (intersections, attachments, headroom) is where the actual design thinking happens.
