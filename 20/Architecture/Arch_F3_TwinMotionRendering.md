# Architectural Design 11 – Twinmotion Rendering

## Unit F: Neighbourhood Site Design & Rendering — Challenge F3

**Time:** 1–1.5 Weeks (4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Bring a combined neighbourhood model into Twinmotion
- Assign and refine materials so three separately-built buildings read consistently
- Produce day and night render sets using sun studies and artificial lighting
- Build a short fly-through walkthrough as a final presentation piece

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What does a scene need before it looks real, beyond just being modeled correctly?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Import & Material Setup (Day 1)

**✏️ Bring the Site into Twinmotion**

1. Export or sync your combined site model (Unit C, D, and E buildings plus Unit F's site and landscape) into Twinmotion.
2. Review how materials imported — flag any that came in incorrectly, missing, or inconsistent between buildings.
3. Reassign or refine materials so the office, villa, and home read consistently under one shared lighting and material system, rather than three visually mismatched exports.
4. Adjust landscape materials (grass, paving, planting) so they match across the whole site.

**Requirements**

- Site successfully imported into Twinmotion
- Materials reviewed and corrected where imported incorrectly
- Materials read consistently across all three buildings and the landscape

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Render Sets & Walkthrough (Days 1–3)

**✏️ Produce Final Renders and a Fly-Through**

1. Set up a day render: adjust the sun study for a believable time of day and check shadows fall sensibly across the site.
2. Set up a night render: adjust artificial lighting (streetlights, building lights) for a believable evening scene.
3. Export both the day and night render sets from at least two camera angles each.
4. Set up a camera path for a short fly-through of the assembled street or intersection, moving past all three buildings.
5. Export the fly-through as a short video and save your project file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_F3_Twinmotion_v01`).

**Requirements**

- A day render set with a believable sun study
- A night render set with believable artificial lighting
- At least two camera angles exported for each render set
- A fly-through video moving past all three buildings

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What material problem came up when you brought the model into Twinmotion, and how did you fix it?
2. What's the difference in how you had to think about lighting for the day render versus the night render?
3. What did the fly-through reveal about the site that a still render didn't?
4. If you were presenting this to a client, which single image or moment from the walkthrough would you lead with, and why?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Material Setup (25%)**

- Materials corrected and consistent across the whole site

**Day & Night Renders (35%)**

- Believable sun study and shadows
- Believable artificial lighting at night

**Fly-Through (20%)**

- Smooth camera path passing all three buildings
- Video exported successfully

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific account of a material or lighting problem solved
- Thoughtful presentation choice explained

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A correctly modeled building isn't automatically a believable one — lighting, materials, and motion are what convince a viewer they're looking at a real place, and that's a separate skill from modeling itself.
