## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Apply exterior material libraries (siding, stone, brick) to a Revit model
- Apply interior material libraries (flooring, paint, trim) to at least two interior spaces
- Use a quick render preview to evaluate material choices before committing to them
- Compare material combinations and justify a final choice

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How do you know a material choice is "working" before you commit to it?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Exterior Materials (Day 1)

**✏️ Apply and Test Exterior Finishes**

1. Open your Island Villa file and select the exterior wall types that need material assignment.
2. Apply at least two different exterior material combinations (e.g., siding + stone accent, or brick + trim color) to wall types or wall faces.
3. Run a quick interactive ray-trace or realistic view to preview each combination.
4. Compare the two exterior options side by side and note which reads better and why.
5. Commit to one exterior material combination for the final model.

**Requirements**

- At least two exterior material combinations tested
- A quick render preview used to evaluate each combination
- One final exterior material combination committed to the model

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Interior Materials (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Apply and Test Interior Finishes**

1. Choose at least two interior spaces (e.g., kitchen and living room) and apply flooring materials to each.
2. Apply paint or wall finish materials to the same spaces.
3. Preview both spaces using a realistic or ray-trace view.
4. Adjust any material that reads poorly (too dark, wrong scale, clashing with an adjacent finish) and re-preview.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_C3_Materials_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Flooring and wall finish materials applied in at least two interior spaces
- A render preview used to check each space
- At least one material adjusted after reviewing the preview

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which exterior combination did you choose, and why did it read better than the alternative?
2. What changed between your first material pass and your final one?
3. What's one thing a quick render preview showed you that you wouldn't have caught just looking at material names in a list?
4. Where might a material choice affect more than just appearance (cost, maintenance, climate)?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Exterior Materials (30%)**

- At least two combinations tested and compared
- Final combination applied cleanly across the model

**Interior Materials (30%)**

- Flooring and finishes applied in at least two spaces
- Materials read clearly and appropriately at building scale

**Use of Render Previews (20%)**

- Preview used to evaluate choices before finalizing
- At least one visible adjustment made based on a preview

**Reflection (20%)**

- Clear justification for the final exterior choice
- Specific example of what the preview revealed
- Thoughtful connection to real-world material considerations

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A material name in a dropdown list and a material rendered on a full-scale wall are two different things — always preview before you commit, because scale, lighting, and adjacency change how a finish actually reads.
