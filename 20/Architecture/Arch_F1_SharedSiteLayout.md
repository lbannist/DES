# Architectural Design 11 – Shared Site Layout

## Unit F: Neighbourhood Site Design & Rendering — Challenge F1

**Time:** 1 Week (3 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Lay out a shared civil site (road, sidewalks, lot lines) for multiple buildings
- Place the Unit C, D, and E buildings onto one site at true scale and correct setbacks
- Resolve scale and orientation conflicts between separately-modeled buildings
- Understand how a single building relates to a street and to its neighbours

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What has to be true for three separately-built models to look like they belong on the same street?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Site & Road Layout (Day 1)

**✏️ Build the Shared Civil Layout**

1. Create a topo surface or site plan representing a short stretch of road, an intersection, or a corner of a neighbourhood.
2. Lay out the road, sidewalks, and lot lines, deciding how many lots your site will contain and their approximate dimensions.
3. Mark required setbacks (front, side, rear) for each lot based on a typical residential/commercial zoning pattern.
4. Confirm the road and sidewalks are at a believable width and the lots are large enough to hold their assigned buildings.

**Requirements**

- A site with road, sidewalks, and defined lot lines
- Setbacks marked for each lot
- Lot sizes reasonable for the buildings being placed

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Placing the Buildings (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Bring In and Position the Unit C, D, and E Models**

1. Link or import your Island Villa (Unit C), custom home (Unit D), and office (Unit E) models into the site file.
2. Position each building on its lot, checking true scale against the road and sidewalks — a building that looked right on its own can read wrong once placed next to a road.
3. Check orientation: does each building's front door face the street sensibly? Adjust rotation as needed.
4. Resolve any scale or unit conflicts between the three source files (all models should share the same project units and be correctly linked/positioned).
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_F1_SiteLayout_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- All three buildings placed on their lots at true scale
- Each building correctly oriented toward the street
- No scale or unit conflicts between the linked models

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What scale or orientation problem came up when you placed your buildings on the shared site, and how did you fix it?
2. Did any of your three buildings feel too big, too small, or oddly proportioned once placed next to the others? What did that tell you?
3. Why does a building's relationship to the street matter as much as its own design?
4. What would you check first if you were adding a fourth building to this site?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Site Layout (30%)**

- Road, sidewalks, lot lines, and setbacks correctly established

**Building Placement (40%)**

- All three buildings placed at true scale with correct orientation
- No scale or unit conflicts between linked models

**Technical Execution (10%)**

- Site file organized cleanly with all links correctly positioned

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific account of a scale/orientation problem and its fix
- Clear understanding of why site context matters to building design

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A building isn't finished when its own model looks right — it's finished when it holds up next to a road, a sidewalk, and its neighbours, because that's the test a real building actually has to pass.
