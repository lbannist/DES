## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Relocate an existing building model onto a new, more constrained site
- Adjust a foundation and grading to respond to a sloped or tight lot
- Understand walkout levels and how site conditions change a floor plan's foundation
- Recognize that a floor plan is not independent of the site it sits on

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If the ground under your house isn't flat, what has to change?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Site Setup (Day 1)

**✏️ Build a Constrained Site**

1. Create or import a topo surface with a meaningful grade change (at least a few feet of elevation change across the building footprint) or define a tight lot with reduced setbacks.
2. Place your Island Villa model from Challenge C1/C2/C3 onto this site.
3. Check where the building currently sits relative to the topo surface — is it floating above grade, or cutting into it awkwardly?
4. Identify which side of the building faces the low point of the slope, since this typically determines where a walkout level makes sense.

**Requirements**

- A topo surface with a real grade change, or a defined tight/constrained lot
- The villa model placed on the site
- The building's relationship to grade identified and documented (floating, cutting in, etc.)

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Foundation & Grading Response (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Adjust the Building to Fit the Site**

1. Adjust foundation wall heights so the building properly responds to the slope (e.g., a walkout basement on the downhill side, or a stepped foundation).
2. Use the Split Surface or grading tools to shape the ground around the building so it reads as built into the site, not placed on top of it.
3. Check any exterior doors or windows affected by the new grade — adjust sill heights or add steps/decks as needed.
4. Review the building in section to confirm the foundation, grade, and walkout level all line up correctly.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_C4_SiteAdaptation_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Foundation adjusted to respond to the site's grade change
- Grading shaped around the building so it reads as sited, not floating
- Affected doors, windows, or exterior elements adjusted for the new grade
- Section view confirms foundation and grade line up correctly

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What specifically had to change about the foundation once the site wasn't flat?
2. Where did you run into a conflict between the original floor plan and the new site (a window too low, a door landing in mid-air, etc.), and how did you resolve it?
3. Why might a walkout basement be a desirable feature rather than just a technical fix?
4. If you were designing a home from scratch for this site instead of adapting one, what would you do differently from the start?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Site & Foundation Response (35%)**

- Foundation correctly adjusted for the site's grade change
- Grading reads as built into the site, not placed on top

**Resolved Conflicts (30%)**

- Doors, windows, and exterior elements correctly adjusted for the new grade
- No floating or buried elements left unresolved

**Technical Execution (15%)**

- Section view confirms foundation, grade, and walkout level align correctly
- Model is clean and well-organized

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific, accurate account of what had to change and why
- Clear understanding of the walkout concept
- Thoughtful idea for designing site-first rather than adapting after the fact

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A floor plan is only ever half the design — the other half is the ground it sits on, and ignoring that until the end always costs more rework than designing with the site from the start.
