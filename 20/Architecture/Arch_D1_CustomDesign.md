# Architectural Design 11 – Design Development

## Unit D: Custom Home Design & Documentation — Challenge D1

**Time:** 1–1.5 Weeks (4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Design a custom home from a client brief or a found floor plan, adapted to fit a real program
- Refine room adjacencies, circulation, and proportions beyond their Grade 10 custom house
- Justify design decisions against a stated program (bedroom count, square footage, style)
- Produce a design that is ready to be documented in the challenges that follow

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What's the difference between a floor plan that "works" and one that's actually designed?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Program & Concept (Day 1)

**✏️ Define the Brief and Block Out a Concept**

1. Choose a client brief or a found floor plan to adapt, and list its program requirements (bedroom/bathroom count, target square footage, style, any special spaces).
2. Sketch two or three rough bubble diagrams by hand or in Revit massing, testing different arrangements of public vs. private zones.
3. Choose one concept to develop and note why it fits the program better than the alternatives.
4. Set up Levels and a rough exterior wall shell in Revit based on the chosen concept.

**Requirements**

- A documented program list (rooms, approximate sizes, special requirements)
- At least two bubble diagram or massing options considered
- One concept selected and justified
- A starting Revit shell in place

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Full Floor Plan Development (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Model the Complete Home**

1. Lay out all interior walls, rooms, and circulation according to your chosen concept.
2. Place doors and windows, checking that room proportions and adjacencies make sense (kitchen near dining, bedrooms away from noise, etc.).
3. Refine the plan at least once based on a walkthrough of the 3D model — look for awkward circulation, wasted space, or rooms that don't read at the right scale.
4. Confirm total square footage against your original program and adjust if it's significantly over or under target.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_D1_CustomHome_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Complete interior layout with all program spaces included
- Doors and windows placed with reasonable adjacencies
- At least one documented refinement made after reviewing the 3D model
- Final square footage checked against the program

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What was your program, and how well does your final plan meet it?
2. What changed between your first concept and your final plan, and why?
3. Where did you have to trade off between two good options (e.g., a bigger kitchen vs. a bigger living room)?
4. What's one adjacency or proportion decision you're most proud of?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Program & Concept (25%)**

- Clear, complete program list
- Multiple concepts considered before one was chosen

**Floor Plan Development (35%)**

- Complete, well-organized interior layout
- Reasonable adjacencies and room proportions
- Evidence of refinement after reviewing the 3D model

**Technical Execution (20%)**

- Model is clean, doors/windows placed correctly
- Square footage reasonably close to program target

**Reflection (20%)**

- Clear articulation of program vs. outcome
- Honest account of design trade-offs made

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A floor plan that technically fits everyone's bedroom isn't the same as a floor plan that's designed — the difference is in the adjacencies, the proportions, and the trade-offs you can explain.
