# Architectural Design 11 – Feature Wall Detailing

## Unit E: Commercial Space Planning (Office Build) — Challenge E2

**Time:** 1 Week (2–3 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Sketch and build a custom 2D profile family in Revit
- Apply that profile as a wall sweep or reveal on a feature wall
- Understand the difference between using a stock component and building a custom detail
- Evaluate a custom detail's proportions and placement at true scale

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What can you build once you stop relying only on Revit's built-in components?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Profile Family (Day 1)

**✏️ Sketch and Load a Custom Profile**

1. Open a new Profile family template and sketch a trim or reveal profile (a simple stepped, curved, or reveal-line shape works well for a first attempt).
2. Dimension the profile so its proportions are intentional, not arbitrary.
3. Save and load the profile family into your office project.
4. Review the profile at true scale next to a standard wall to check its proportions read well.

**Requirements**

- A custom profile family sketched and dimensioned
- Profile saved and loaded into the office project
- Proportions checked at true scale before applying

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Apply to the Feature Wall (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Build the Feature Wall**

1. Choose a wall in your office (reception or a key open-area wall works well) to become the feature wall.
2. Apply your custom profile as a wall sweep or reveal, positioning it appropriately (a reveal line pattern, a base/cap trim, or a full accent band).
3. Adjust the profile's placement, spacing, or repetition until it reads intentionally on the wall, not randomly placed.
4. Render a quick preview of the feature wall to check how it reads with lighting and material.
5. Save your file using a version-numbered name (e.g., `LastName_E2_FeatureWall_v01.rvt`).

**Requirements**

- Custom profile applied as a sweep or reveal on a chosen feature wall
- Placement/spacing intentional, not arbitrary
- A render preview showing the finished feature wall

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What was harder than expected about building your own profile family?
2. How did seeing the profile at true scale change your design (if at all)?
3. Why might a designer choose a custom detail over a stock trim component?
4. Where else in a project might a custom family be worth building instead of using a stock one?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Profile Family (30%)**

- Custom profile sketched, dimensioned, and correctly loaded

**Feature Wall Application (40%)**

- Sweep/reveal applied cleanly to the chosen wall
- Placement and proportions read as intentional

**Presentation (10%)**

- Render preview clearly shows the finished detail

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific account of what was difficult about building a custom family
- Clear reasoning for when a custom detail is worth the extra effort

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Every stock component in Revit started as someone's custom family — learning to build your own is what turns you from someone who assembles other people's parts into someone who can design new ones.
