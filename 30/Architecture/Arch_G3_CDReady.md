# Architectural Design 12 – Design Development to CD-Ready

## Unit G: Competition Design Challenge (BILD-style) — Challenge G3

**Time:** 2 Weeks (6–8 Class Periods, Fixed Competition-Style Deadline)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date — treat as a hard deadline, no extensions]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Take a selected concept (from G2) to a fully documented drawing set under real time pressure
- Practice prioritizing work when time runs out before polish does — a core competition and professional skill
- Produce a coordinated, competition-ready set: floor plans, elevations, section, and a presentation sheet
- Manage their own time against a fixed deadline the way a professional team would

---

## 💭 Production Question

### When the clock runs out, what's the difference between a set that looks unfinished and a set that looks intentionally scoped?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Work-Back Schedule & Plan Finalization (Day 1)

**✏️ Plan Before You Model**

1. Build a work-back schedule from the fixed deadline, allocating time blocks to floor plans, elevations, section, materials/rendering, and sheet assembly.
2. Finalize your schematic floor plan into a fully walled, dimensioned plan for every level.
3. Identify the one or two features from your concept that are non-negotiable for the jury to see, and flag them as priorities in your schedule.

**Requirements**

- A written work-back schedule covering the full G3 timeline
- Finalized, fully dimensioned floor plan(s)
- Non-negotiable design features identified

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Elevations & Section (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Document the Building's Exterior and Interior Logic**

1. Generate all primary exterior elevations, correctly annotated and dimensioned.
2. Cut and generate at least one building section showing floor-to-floor heights and key structural/spatial relationships.
3. Check every view against your finalized floor plan for consistency (no floating walls, mismatched openings, or impossible conditions).

**Requirements**

- All primary elevations generated and annotated
- At least one building section generated
- Cross-checked consistency between plans, elevations, and section

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Materials, Presentation Graphics & Sheet Assembly (Days 5–7)

**✏️ Assemble the Set You'd Actually Submit**

1. Apply exterior materials and produce at least one presentation-quality 3D view or render.
2. Assemble a competition-style presentation sheet (or small set) combining plans, elevations, section, and the 3D view with a title block and project narrative blurb.
3. Proofread the set for missing dimensions, mislabeled views, and inconsistent scales.

**Requirements**

- Materials applied and at least one presentation-quality 3D view produced
- A coordinated, assembled presentation sheet/set
- Evidence of a proofreading/consistency pass

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Did you meet your own work-back schedule? Where did you fall behind or get ahead?
2. What did you deliberately cut or simplify to meet the deadline, and why?
3. How did your non-negotiable features from Checkpoint 1 survive into the final set?
4. What would you do differently if you had this exact deadline again?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Planning & Time Management (15%)**

- Realistic, complete work-back schedule
- Clear prioritization of non-negotiable features

**Documentation Completeness (35%)**

- Fully dimensioned plans, elevations, and section
- Cross-view consistency

**Presentation Quality (30%)**

- Coordinated, professional-looking sheet assembly
- Effective materials/render supporting the design intent

**Reflection (20%)**

- Honest, specific account of time management and trade-offs

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A fixed deadline doesn't test how much you can model — it tests what you choose to finish, and a scoped, coordinated set beats an ambitious, incomplete one every time.
