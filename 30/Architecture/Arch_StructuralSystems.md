# Architectural Design 12 – Structural Systems Intro

## Unit H: Advanced Documentation & Building Systems — Challenge H1

**Time:** 1 Week (3–4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Understand the basic components of a residential structural system (foundation, floor framing, wall framing, roof framing)
- Model a basic foundation and framing system in Revit
- Produce a framing/foundation plan that reads like a real construction document
- Recognize how structural decisions constrain and enable architectural design

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If the drywall and finishes disappeared, would your building still stand up the way you drew it?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Foundation System (Day 1)

**✏️ Model What the Building Actually Sits On**

1. Using an existing or new custom home model, research the typical foundation type for your building's context (full basement, crawlspace, slab-on-grade) and choose one to model.
2. Model the foundation walls and footings in Revit at the correct depth and thickness for your chosen type.
3. Add a foundation plan view showing footing locations, foundation wall thickness, and any pads or piers.

**Requirements**

- A modeled foundation system appropriate to the building
- A labeled foundation plan view

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Floor & Wall Framing (Day 2)

**✏️ Build the Skeleton**

1. Model the floor framing system for at least one level (joists or trusses, at correct spacing and direction).
2. Model or represent the wall framing system (studs at standard spacing) for at least one exterior wall and one load-bearing interior wall.
3. Note where floor framing direction changes and why (based on span and support conditions).

**Requirements**

- Floor framing modeled for at least one level, correctly spaced and directed
- Wall framing represented for at least two walls (one exterior, one interior load-bearing)
- A short written note explaining a framing direction decision

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Roof Framing & Framing Plan (Day 3)

**✏️ Close It In**

1. Model a basic roof framing system (rafters or trusses) consistent with your roof form.
2. Produce a combined framing/foundation plan sheet showing foundation, floor framing, and roof framing information, each clearly labeled.
3. Cross-check that your structural elements don't conflict with your architectural plan (no joists running through a stairwell opening, no wall resting on nothing below it).

**Requirements**

- Roof framing modeled
- A combined, clearly labeled framing/foundation plan
- A documented consistency check between structure and architecture

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What foundation and framing choices did you make, and why did you choose them over the alternatives?
2. Where did you find a conflict between your architectural design and your structural system, and how did you resolve it?
3. What's one thing about "how buildings actually stand up" that surprised you?
4. How will thinking about structure change how you design in H2 and beyond?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Foundation System (25%)**

- Appropriate foundation type chosen and justified
- Accurately modeled and labeled

**Floor & Wall Framing (30%)**

- Correct spacing, direction, and structural logic
- Load-bearing vs. non-load-bearing distinction evident

**Roof Framing & Plan Documentation (25%)**

- Roof framing consistent with roof form
- Combined framing/foundation plan is clear and correctly labeled

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific, thoughtful account of structural decisions and conflicts resolved

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A floor plan is a promise about how a building will be built — structural thinking is what makes sure that promise can actually be kept.
