# Architectural Design 12 – Presentation Boards & Mock Jury

## Unit G: Competition Design Challenge (BILD-style) — Challenge G3

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods, Fixed Competition-Style Deadline)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date — treat as a hard deadline, no extensions]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Turn a completed design into presentation boards that tell a clear story, moving from the general to the specific
- Plan a board layout that gives the jury's most important information first
- Prepare print-ready boards and a submission package that meet the competition's technical requirements
- Audit their own work against a compliance checklist the way a jury (and a disqualification rule) would
- Give and receive critique in a mock jury, and respond to feedback with targeted revisions

---

## 💭 Production Question

### A jury spends only minutes on each entry. What does your board need to say, in what order, to win those minutes?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Board Story & Layout Planning (Day 1)

**✏️ Plan the Story Before You Place a Single Image**

1. Review the competition's board size, board count, and required content for your declared level (from your G2 level declaration).
2. Study a few example layouts (for example, the *Composition in Presentation Boards* video) and note what makes each one effective.
3. Decide the story your boards will tell, moving from general to specific (for example: project name and idea, then site and diagrams, then plans, then elevations, then renders).
4. Sketch **two or three thumbnail layouts** showing where each required item will go on each board.
5. Choose one layout and note why it works for your design.

**Requirements**

- A list of required board content for your level
- Two or three thumbnail layout sketches
- A chosen layout with a one- or two-sentence rationale

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Graphics, Renders & Board Text (Day 2)

**✏️ Create the Content the Boards Need**

1. Finish and export the views you need from Revit (plans, elevations, section, site plan, perspective/axonometric/interior) at a resolution suitable for large-format printing.
2. Create or refine renders. Your main focal point render should be higher quality; supporting images can be simpler.
3. Create any diagrams that help tell the story (site, sun/light, circulation, water feature, or concept).
4. Write the project name and a short project description in your own words.
5. Reuse your Design Vision, Mood Board, or Precedent ideas where they strengthen the story (optional).

**Requirements**

- All required views exported at print-ready resolution
- At least one high-quality focal render
- Project name and description written
- At least one supporting diagram

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Board Assembly & Technical Export (Day 3)

**✏️ Assemble the Boards You'd Actually Submit**

1. Build your boards to the required size in Canva, InDesign, or Revit sheets (your choice — use what you can finish well).
2. Place all content following your chosen layout. Make sure all required drawings are present, readable, and labelled.
3. Note the **scale** on every drawing (plans, elevations, and perspectives), and confirm the scales meet the package requirements.
4. Export your boards as PDF, checking resolution and legibility at full size.
5. Prepare your Revit file: confirm it contains the entire design in one file, with clearly named views and sheets, and no stray or unused elements.

**Requirements**

- Assembled boards at the required size and board count
- Scale noted on every drawing
- PDF export of all boards
- One Revit file containing the entire design

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Compliance Audit & Mock Jury (Day 4)

**✏️ Be the Jury, Then Face the Jury**

1. **Self-audit:** Go through your G1 compliance checklist line by line against your final boards and Revit file. Mark each item as met, partly met, or missing.
2. **Peer audit:** Swap boards with a classmate. Using the checklist and the scoring priorities from G1, check their boards for missing items, scale errors, and unclear information.
3. **Mock jury:** Present your boards to a small panel (classmates and your teacher). Jurors should bring scales and calculators and check at least one square footage calculation and one dimension on each entry.
4. Jurors give feedback on layout, story, design uniqueness, traffic flow, accuracy of elevations, and presentation quality.
5. Record all feedback in a **revision log** listing what you will change, what you will not change, and why.

**Requirements**

- A completed self-audit checklist
- A peer audit with specific, helpful notes
- Participation as both presenter and juror
- A revision log based on jury feedback

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Final Revisions & Submission (Day 5)

**✏️ Fix What Matters Most, Then Submit**

1. Prioritize your revision log. Fix compliance issues first, then accuracy issues, then layout and polish.
2. Make your revisions in Revit and in your board files.
3. Run a final proofread: check spelling, title blocks, scales, dimensions, and missing labels.
4. Re-export your boards as PDF and save your final Revit file.
5. Submit your final package and complete the reflection below.

**Reflection**

1. What did the mock jury notice that you had missed, and what did you change as a result?
2. If you had one more day, what would you improve first?

**Requirements**

- Revisions completed in priority order
- Final proofread completed
- Final boards (PDF) and Revit file submitted
- Two reflection answers

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Board Story & Layout (30%)**

- Clear story from general to specific
- Layout is organized, balanced, and easy to read
- Most important information is easiest to find

**Graphics, Renders & Technical Presentation (20%)**

- High-quality focal render and clean supporting images
- All drawings at print-ready resolution, with scale noted
- Effective use of colour, diagrams, and text

**Compliance Audit & Jury Response (30%)**

- Self-audit and peer audit are thorough and accurate
- Constructive participation in the mock jury
- Revision log shows thoughtful, prioritized responses to feedback
- Final submission meets all package requirements for the declared level

**Submission Package & Reflection (20%)**

- Correct file formats, sizes, and filenames
- Revit file is complete and clearly organized
- Honest, specific reflection

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A design only wins if the jury can see it. Boards that tell a clear story, meet every requirement, and show the strongest idea first beat a better design that is hard to read or breaks a rule.
