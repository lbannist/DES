# Architectural Design 12 – Brief Analysis & Design Vision

## Unit G: Competition Design Challenge (BILD-style) — Challenge G1

**Time:** 1 Week (4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Read and interpret a real competition package the way a practicing design team would before starting design
- Extract a complete client program (spatial list, square footages, target buyer/market)
- Separate hard constraints (site, zoning, budget, code, sustainability targets) from soft preferences (style, "nice-to-haves")
- Build a compliance checklist from the package's rules so the entry cannot be disqualified on a technicality
- Identify how the jury will actually score the entry, and what that means for where design effort should go
- Develop and communicate a design direction visually using a Design Vision, Mood Board, and Precedent Sheet

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If you only had one hour with the client, what would you need to ask them — and where does the package already answer that for you?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — First Read & Annotation (Day 1)

**✏️ Read the Whole Package Before You Design Anything**

1. Read the assigned past BILD (or similar) competition package start to finish without sketching anything yet.
2. Annotate the package (digitally or on a printout) highlighting: program requirements, site information, code/regulatory notes, and anything unclear.
3. Flag at least three items that would need clarification if you could ask the client a question.
4. Make a **provisional** choice of building location (if the package offers options) and, where relevant, the site feature or "must-have" element your design will respond to. You can change this later, but you need a starting point for Day 2.

**Requirements**

- Fully annotated package (digital markup or scanned printout)
- At least three flagged clarification questions
- A provisional site/feature choice noted on the package

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Design Vision, Mood Board & Precedent Sheet (Day 2)

**✏️ Give Your Design a Direction You Can See**

Before any hard drafting begins, designers establish *what the building should feel like*. Build three separate 8.5" x 11" sheets in **Canva** (or a similar layout tool of your choice). **Krita** is optional if you want to paint a quick site diagram or sketch a concept to include.

**Sheet 1 — Design Vision**

- A one-sentence design vision statement
- Three style keywords
- Your chosen site/location and feature, with a short note on how the house will "experience" it

**Sheet 2 — Mood Board**

- 6–8 images covering material textures, a colour palette, and the atmosphere or interior feel you are aiming for
- Images should reflect the architectural style required by the package
- Include a short caption or label for each image group (e.g., "Materials," "Palette," "Atmosphere")

**Sheet 3 — Precedent Sheet**

- Two **built** projects (or past competition entries) with a similar program, site type, or style
- Each precedent has: the project name, an image, and a caption naming the **one idea** you are borrowing and why

**Image credits:** Include a small-text source credit for every image used.

**Teacher check-in:** At the end of class, show your three sheets for a quick direction check.

**Requirements**

- Three completed 8.5" x 11" sheets: Design Vision, Mood Board, Precedent Sheet
- Source credits for all images
- Evidence the style direction matches the package requirements

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Program & Constraint Extraction (Day 3)

**✏️ Build Your Program Document**

1. Extract a room-by-room program list: spaces, approximate square footages, and any relationships called for (e.g., "ensuite off primary bedroom," "mudroom adjacent to garage").
2. List all hard constraints separately: lot dimensions/setbacks, height restrictions, applicable code requirements, budget or cost targets, sustainability or accessibility requirements, and any required "must-haves."
3. List soft preferences separately: style direction, "wow factor" asks, optional upgrades.
4. State the total target square footage and check it against the site's buildable envelope. Use the package's stated method for measuring area (for example, measuring from the outside of walls and counting stairs on every floor).
5. Build a **compliance checklist** from the package: every rule that could lead to disqualification or a change of entry level (required rooms, room counts, required drawings, drawing scales, board sizes and counts, file formats, and submission rules).
6. **Vision check:** Review your Day 2 sheets against your constraints. Does your vision still fit? Note any adjustments you need to make.

**Requirements**

- A complete program list with square footages
- Hard constraints and soft preferences clearly separated
- A stated buildable envelope check (does the program plausibly fit the site?)
- A compliance checklist covering all disqualification and submission rules
- A short note on whether your vision needs adjusting

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Scoring Breakdown & Wrap-Up (Day 4)

**✏️ Reverse-Engineer the Jury, Then Finish Your Package**

1. Locate the competition's judging/scoring criteria (or, if not provided, infer a reasonable weighting from the package's emphasis) and rank which categories carry the most weight.
2. Summarize, in your own words, the three things your design absolutely cannot get wrong to score well.
3. Finalize and export your three Day 2 sheets as **one 3-page PDF**.
4. Answer the two reflection questions below.

**Reflection**

1. Which constraint do you expect to be the hardest to design around, and why?
2. What is the single clarifying question you would most want answered before starting concept design?

**Requirements**

- A scoring breakdown or inferred priority ranking
- A short list of the top three "cannot get wrong" priorities
- Final 3-page Vision PDF exported
- Two reflection answers

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Package Comprehension (20%)**

- Thorough, accurate annotation
- Meaningful clarification questions identified

**Program & Constraint Extraction (35%)**

- Complete, accurate program list with square footages
- Hard constraints and soft preferences correctly separated
- Buildable envelope check completed
- Compliance checklist is complete and accurate
- Reasonable, well-justified scoring priority ranking with top three priorities

**Design Vision, Mood Board & Precedent Sheets (30%)**

- Clear, specific vision statement and style keywords
- Mood board communicates materials, palette, and atmosphere
- Two relevant precedents, each with a clear takeaway
- Style direction fits the package requirements
- Clean layout, readable text, and image credits included

**Reflection (15%)**

- Thoughtful, specific answers connecting analysis to next steps

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Every competition is won or lost before the first line is drawn — the teams that read the brief like a client conversation, not a checklist, and who can show what they are aiming for, are the ones whose concepts actually answer the real question being asked.
