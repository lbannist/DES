## 📋 Project Overview

In this project, the land tells you how to build. You will choose a real site, study everything about it — the sun, wind, slope, views, and neighbours — and then design a building that belongs there. Every major decision in your design should be traceable back to what the site taught you. A well thought out design can often make the building blend into the landscape and make it appear like it was always meant to be there.

## Phase 1 — Site Selection and Initial Investigation

### What You're Doing
You're choosing your site. Think about what kind of site genuinely interests you — a steep hillside, a narrow city lot squeezed between buildings, a lakeside acreage, or a corner lot in an established neighbourhood. Your site can be real or plausible, but it should feel grounded and specific.

### Your Site Inventory Should Include
- Lot dimensions and shape
- Topography (flat, sloped, terraced?)
- Existing vegetation — trees, shrubs, grass
- Neighbouring structures and their heights
- Street access and direction
- Any water features, drainage ditches, or natural elements

> 💡 **Tip:** Google Maps satellite view and Google Earth are great tools for studying a real site. Many municipalities also have free GIS (mapping) tools online where you can find lot dimensions, zoning, and topography data.

### ✅ Phase 1 Deliverables
- [ ] Written site selection rationale
- [ ] Base site plan
- [ ] Site inventory notes

## Phase 2 — Site Analysis

### What You're Doing
Phase 1 told you *what is there*. Phase 2 answers the question: *what does it mean?*

### Six Things to Investigate

**☀️ Sun and Shadow** — Where does the sun rise and set relative to your lot? Where will shadows fall from neighbouring buildings or trees? This affects where you place living spaces, windows, and outdoor areas.

**💨 Wind** — What direction do prevailing winds come from? This affects entry placement, natural ventilation, and where outdoor spaces will actually be comfortable.

**🌊 Topography and Drainage** — How does water move across and off your site? This shapes where the building sits, how the foundation is handled, and whether you need retaining walls or grading.

**👁️ Views and Privacy** — What does your client want to look at, and what do they want screened from?

**🚗 Access and Circulation** — Where do people arrive from? Where does parking go without dominating the design?

**🏘️ Context and Character** — What does the surrounding neighbourhood look, feel, and function like?

> 💡 **Tip:** Your main deliverable for this phase is an **annotated site analysis drawing** — take your base site plan and layer on arrows, notes, symbols, and diagrams showing sun paths, wind direction, views, noise sources, and more.

### ✅ Phase 2 Deliverables
- [ ] Annotated site analysis drawing
- [ ] Written summary of key findings and what they mean for your design

## Phase 3 — Design Brief, Client Profile & Foundation Thinking

### What You're Doing
Now that you understand your site, you decide who is building there and why — and start thinking about how the building actually meets the ground.

### Creating Your Client Profile
Consider who your client is *(age, family structure, occupation, lifestyle)*, how they use their home, any accessibility needs, cultural background, aesthetic preferences, and their relationship to the outdoors.

### Writing Your Design Brief
Your brief should include client needs and priorities, accessibility requirements, aesthetic preferences, special programmatic needs, general budget considerations, and any site-specific preferences.

### Building Program
A room-by-room list of every space your design needs, with approximate size in square metres and key relationships *(e.g., "mudroom should connect directly to garage and kitchen")*.

### Foundation & Structural System *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit H skill)*
Using your Phase 2 topography findings and your Unit H structural knowledge, decide on and justify a basic foundation and framing approach appropriate to your site: a standard foundation on flat ground, a stepped or walkout foundation on a slope, or a pier/crawlspace system where drainage or grade makes sense of it. Note your approximate structural bay/framing layout — you don't need a full structural drawing set, but you do need a defensible choice.

### Alberta Building Code Notes *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit H skill)*
Cite the Alberta Building Code sections relevant to your project's occupancy and site: minimum egress requirements for your structure type, any accessibility requirements that apply, and — if your site is sloped — any implications for grade-related egress or exiting.

### ✅ Phase 3 Deliverables
- [ ] Written client profile
- [ ] Written design brief covering all requirements
- [ ] Building program
- [ ] Foundation/structural approach, justified against site conditions
- [ ] Alberta Building Code notes citing applicable sections

## Phase 4 — Concept Development

### What You're Doing
This is where you start designing — but not by going straight to the final answer. **Volume before refinement.**

### How to Do Thumbnail Sketches
Each thumbnail should show where the building sits on the lot, the general shape and massing, which direction the main windows face, how the building relates to the street and neighbours, and any interesting moves — does it step with the slope? Does it have a courtyard?

### Questions to Push Your Thinking
- Where does the building sit on the lot, and why there specifically?
- How does it address the street?
- Where are the main glazed faces and why? *(Remember your sun analysis!)*
- Does the building step with or push against the topography?
- Is it one form or multiple connected volumes?

> 💡 **Tip:** Annotate your thumbnails! *"South-facing glazing for passive solar"* or *"garage pushed to north to shield from wind"* — these notes show that your design is responding to your site analysis.

### Evaluate Before You Commit *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit G skill)*
Just like you did in G2, build a short comparison scoring each thumbnail direction against your brief's stated priorities (site response, program fit, structural feasibility, code feasibility). Select one direction and write a rationale that explicitly references that comparison.

### ✅ Phase 4 Deliverables
- [ ] Minimum 4–6 thumbnail concept sketches with annotations
- [ ] A comparison of thumbnail directions against your brief's priorities
- [ ] Written concept selection rationale

## Phase 5 — Design Development & Professional Documentation

### What You're Doing
With your concept chosen, you develop it into a complete, professionally formatted set of architectural drawings — the set a contractor would actually use to build your design.

### Floor Plan
**Scale: 1:50 or 1:100.** All walls, doors *(shown swinging open)*, windows, stairs, and major built-ins. Every room labeled with overall dimensions. Layout should reflect your site analysis — living areas oriented toward the sun, bedrooms away from street noise, etc.

### Elevations *(minimum 2 — front + one other)*
Material callouts required, along with any outdoor elements like decks, overhangs, or screen walls.

### Building Section
**Scale: 1:50 or 1:100.** Show interior ceiling heights, floor-to-floor relationships, roof structure, and — importantly — how the building responds to any grade changes on your site, consistent with the foundation approach you chose in Phase 3.

### Site Plan
**Scale: 1:200 or 1:500.** Building footprint within the full lot, driveway, walkways, landscaping, outdoor living areas, parking, significant natural features, north arrow, and scale bar.

### Detail Drawing *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit H skill)*
Pick one condition in your design that deserves a closer look — a foundation-to-grade condition on a slope, a stair, or a wall assembly at a key window — and draft it as a **wall section or detail at a larger scale**, the same way you did in Unit H.

### Professional Sheet Set *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit H skill)*
Assemble your floor plan, elevations, section, detail, and site plan into a **coordinated CD-style sheet set**: a consistent title block on every sheet, a logical sheet numbering system (e.g., A0.1 cover, A1.1 floor plan, A2.1 elevations, A3.1 section, A4.1 detail, A5.1 site plan), and a cover sheet with a drawing index.

### ✅ Phase 5 Deliverables
- [ ] Detailed floor plan *(scaled, dimensioned, labeled)*
- [ ] Minimum two elevations
- [ ] Building section reflecting your foundation/grade response
- [ ] Site plan
- [ ] One wall section or detail drawing at a larger scale
- [ ] All drawings assembled into a coordinated sheet set with title block, sheet numbering, and a cover sheet/index

## Phase 6 — Physical Model, Digital Presentation & Portfolio

### 3D Printed Model — Part A: Floor Plan with Printed Walls

#### 🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Export & Print Pipeline (Unit I skill)
Model your walls directly in Revit at 1:1. **Export directly from Revit as an STL or OBJ**, exactly as you did in Unit I. Bring the file into **Elegoo Slicer or Orca Slicer**, scale to **1:100**, and check wall thickness (minimum 3–4 mm) and any thin overhangs before slicing. You don't need to print every interior partition, but include all exterior walls and any major interior walls that define the main spaces.

Prepare a clean printed floor plan base at the same scale, mounted on a rigid base *(foam core or MDF)*.

> 💡 **Tip:** Consider two filament colours to distinguish exterior walls from interior partitions — white for exterior, grey for interior.

### 3D Printed Model — Part B: Scaled Site Model

Print your site plan at 1:200 or 1:500 on a rigid base. 3D print a simplified massing model of your building, exported the same way as Part A, showing the overall form and roofline. Represent topography with layered foam core contours cut from your site analysis, and show trees, driveway, walkways, and outdoor living areas.

### Advanced Twinmotion Presentation *(🎓 Grade 12 Upgrade — Unit I skill)*

Where the original version of this project ended with model photography, your capstone version pairs the physical model with a **digital presentation built in Twinmotion**, at the advanced level from Unit I. Choose one:

- **A day and night render set** of the exterior and one key interior space, showing how your sun/lighting analysis actually plays out, or
- **A short cinematic walkthrough or VR-ready sequence** from arrival at the site through the key interior space

### Final Presentation
Your critique presentation should walk your audience through the site, the client, the concept, the code and structural decisions, and the design. Present your physical model and your Twinmotion output together — the model shows the massing and site fit, the render/walkthrough shows the lived experience.

### Portfolio Compilation
Your portfolio is the complete record of your design process. Include photographs of both models from multiple angles, in-progress photos of printing and assembly, all phase work from site inventory through the final sheet set, your Twinmotion output, and a written reflection on site response, environmental stewardship, and what you'd change.

### ✅ Phase 6 Deliverables
- [ ] 3D printed wall model, exported from Revit and sliced at 1:100, on printed floor plan base
- [ ] Scaled site model with building massing and topography
- [ ] Advanced Twinmotion presentation *(render set or walkthrough)*
- [ ] Final critique presentation
- [ ] Complete portfolio *(all phase work)*
- [ ] Photographs of both models from multiple angles
- [ ] Written reflection

## 🔧 Common Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Walls too thin and snap | Wall thickness below 2 mm at 1:100 | Increase wall thickness in Revit before export, minimum 3–4 mm |
| Model is wrong size when sliced | Units mismatch or scale not applied before export | Confirm scale in Revit and re-check units in Elegoo/Orca Slicer before printing |
| Openings don't line up with floor plan | Scale error between exported model and printed base | Re-check scale calculation; reprint base if needed |
| Print warps or lifts off bed | Bed adhesion issue | Use brim setting in slicer; ensure bed is level and clean |
| Walls won't stay upright on base | Bottom surface not flat or insufficient glue | Sand base of walls flat; clamp while drying |
