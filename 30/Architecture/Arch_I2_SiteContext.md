# Architectural Design 12 – Site/Context Model

## Unit I: Physical & Digital Prototyping — Challenge I2

**Time:** 1 Week (3–4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Translate a site plan into a physical base plate at 1:100 scale
- Represent site features (footprint, driveway, landscape elements) accurately at scale
- Coordinate a printed or built site model with the building massing model produced in I1
- Understand how a site model changes the way a design reads in presentation

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Does your home look like it belongs on its site, or like it was dropped there from somewhere else?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Base Plate Layout (Day 1)

**✏️ Lay Out the Site at Scale**

1. Using your site plan, confirm the site footprint boundaries and scale everything to 1:100 (matching your I1 massing model).
2. Mark the building footprint location on the base plate, along with the driveway and any major hardscape (walkways, patios).
3. Choose a base plate material and method (3D printed base, cut foam core, MDF, or a hybrid) appropriate to your site's shape and any grade changes.

**Requirements**

- Site footprint and building location correctly scaled and marked
- Driveway and major hardscape represented
- Base plate material/method chosen and justified

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Landscape Elements (Day 2)

**✏️ Add What Makes the Site Read**

1. Add key landscape elements at scale: trees, planting beds, fencing, or any significant natural features from your site design.
2. If your site has grade change, represent it (layered contours, a sloped base, or another appropriate method).
3. Confirm the building massing model from I1 sits correctly and securely on the completed base.

**Requirements**

- Key landscape elements represented at correct scale
- Grade change represented if applicable
- Building massing model test-fitted onto the base

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Final Assembly (Day 3)

**✏️ Put It Together**

1. Finalize and secure all site elements to the base plate.
2. Permanently or securely place the building massing model in its correct location and orientation.
3. Photograph the completed site/context model from multiple angles, including at least one photo showing scale relative to a common object (ruler, coin, etc.).

**Requirements**

- Fully assembled, secured site/context model
- Building model correctly placed and oriented
- Photo documentation from multiple angles with a scale reference

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What site feature made the biggest difference to how the model reads once it was built?
2. Where did the physical model reveal something about your site design that the digital site plan didn't show clearly?
3. What was hardest to represent accurately at 1:100, and how did you solve it?
4. How does seeing the building on its site change your read of the design compared to the massing model alone?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Site Footprint & Scale Accuracy (25%)**

- Correct scaling matching the building massing model
- Accurate placement of building, driveway, and hardscape

**Landscape & Grade Representation (30%)**

- Key landscape elements represented clearly
- Grade change represented if applicable

**Assembly & Presentation Quality (25%)**

- Clean, secure final assembly
- Clear photo documentation with scale reference

**Reflection (20%)**

- Specific, thoughtful reflection connecting the site model to the design

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A building floating on a blank base isn't finished — a site model is what proves your design actually responds to the place it claims to belong to.
