# Architectural Design 12 – Advanced Twinmotion Presentation

## Unit I: Physical & Digital Prototyping — Challenge I3

**Time:** 1–1.5 Weeks (4 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Produce a polished, advanced-level Twinmotion presentation of a completed design
- Choose between a cinematic render sequence and a VR-ready walkthrough based on what best communicates the design
- Pair a digital presentation with the physical models produced in I1/I2 for a complete final presentation
- Apply lighting, camera, and pacing choices deliberately rather than by default settings

---

## 💭 Production Question

### If someone only ever saw your Twinmotion output and never the real drawings, would they understand your design?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Planning the Presentation (Day 1)

**✏️ Decide What You're Actually Making**

1. Choose your presentation format: a cinematic render sequence (a short pre-rendered fly-through/walkthrough video) or a VR-ready interactive walkthrough.
2. Storyboard or list the key views/moments your presentation needs to include (exterior approach, key interior space, a signature design feature).
3. Import your finished model into Twinmotion and confirm materials, context, and site are set up correctly.

**Requirements**

- Format chosen and justified
- A storyboard or shot list of key views/moments
- Model correctly imported into Twinmotion with materials and context in place

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Lighting & Atmosphere (Day 2)

**✏️ Make Deliberate Choices, Not Default Ones**

1. Set up at least one daytime and one evening/night lighting scenario, adjusting sun position and artificial lighting.
2. Choose which lighting scenario(s) best support your presentation's goals and explain why.
3. Adjust materials as needed so they read correctly under your chosen lighting.

**Requirements**

- At least two distinct lighting scenarios set up
- A justified choice of which scenario(s) to use in the final presentation
- Materials adjusted for correct appearance under lighting

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Sequence/Walkthrough Production (Days 3–4)

**✏️ Build the Final Output**

1. Produce your chosen output: render out a cinematic sequence with deliberate camera movement and pacing, or build and test a VR-ready walkthrough path.
2. Confirm the sequence/walkthrough covers all key views/moments from your Checkpoint 1 shot list.
3. Export the final file in an appropriate format and do a full review for glitches, clipping, or broken materials.

**Requirements**

- A completed render sequence or walkthrough covering all planned key views
- Exported in an appropriate final format
- A full review completed with issues corrected

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Why did you choose a render sequence vs. a walkthrough (or vice versa) for this design specifically?
2. What lighting or camera choice made the biggest difference to how the design reads?
3. How does presenting the design digitally change what you emphasize compared to presenting the physical models from I1/I2?
4. Looking at your physical models and your Twinmotion output side by side, what does each one show that the other can't?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Planning & Format Choice (20%)**

- Well-justified format choice
- Clear, complete shot list/storyboard

**Lighting & Atmosphere (25%)**

**Final Production Quality (35%)**

- Polished, glitch-free final output

- Specific, thoughtful reflection on presentation choices

## 💡 Key Lesson

A render or walkthrough isn't decoration on top of a finished design — done well, it's the moment a client finally understands the space the way you always saw it.
