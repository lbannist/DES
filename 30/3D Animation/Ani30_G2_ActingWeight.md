## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Record and use video reference to ground an animation choice in real, observed motion
- Convey weight through timing and posing choices
- Communicate personality through acting choices, not just accurate motion

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What's the difference between animation that's technically correct and animation that feels like someone is actually there?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Video Reference Recording (Day 1)

**📖 Record Yourself Acting It Out**

1. Choose a short action for your character to perform (picking up a heavy object, reacting to a surprise, sitting down exhausted) — something with a clear weight or emotional component.
2. Record yourself (or a classmate, with permission) physically acting out that action on a phone or webcam, multiple takes if needed.
3. Review your footage frame by frame (scrub slowly) and note the key moments — where does the body anticipate, where's the peak of effort, where does it settle?
4. Identify at least three specific physical details from your reference you want to carry into your animation (a specific hesitation, a particular way the shoulders drop, a head movement).

**Questions to answer:**

1. What action did you choose, and why does it have a clear weight or emotional component?
2. What three specific physical details from your reference do you want to animate?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Blocking with Weight in Mind (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Block Your Animation**

1. Using your Challenge G1 rig, block in the key poses for your action, referring back to your video reference for each major pose.
2. Pay attention to weight distribution in every pose — where is the character's center of gravity, and does the pose look balanced (or intentionally off-balance in a believable way)?
3. Time your key poses to roughly match the timing you observed in your video reference — don't guess; actually compare frame counts if possible.
4. Play back your blocked animation next to your video reference (side by side, or by flipping quickly between windows) and check how closely the timing and weight match.

**Requirements**

- Key poses clearly informed by the recorded video reference
- Weight distribution considered and intentional in each pose
- Timing roughly matched to the reference, not just estimated

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Personality Pass (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Add Character-Specific Acting Choices**

1. Beyond just matching your reference physically, ask: how would THIS specific character (not a generic person) do this action, given their personality established back in Gr10 Challenge A2/A4?
2. Adjust at least one pose or timing choice to reflect the character's personality rather than your own reference performance exactly (e.g., if your character is nervous, add a hesitation your own reference didn't have).
3. Refine secondary details (a hand fidget, a facial reaction, a small overcorrection) that reinforce the character's specific personality.
4. Play back the full animation and check: does this feel like a specific character doing this action, or could it be anyone?

**Requirements**

- At least one deliberate acting choice made that diverges from the literal reference footage, in service of character personality
- Secondary details added that reinforce personality
- Final animation reads as character-specific, not generic

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did recording and reviewing your own reference reveal that you wouldn't have thought of by animating from imagination alone?
2. Where did you choose to deviate from the literal reference to better serve your character's personality?
3. What's the difference between "weight" and "timing," and how did you use each?
4. What's one acting/performance skill you want to push further in Challenge G3's secondary action work?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Reference Use (25%)**

- Video reference genuinely recorded and reviewed in detail
- Specific physical details identified and carried into the animation
- Timing informed by actual observed reference, not guesswork
- Reference footage submitted alongside the final animation

**Weight & Timing (30%)**

- Poses show clear, intentional weight distribution
- Timing choices reflect the physical effort/emotion of the action
- Blocking demonstrates a real understanding of how weight shows up in motion
- No floaty or mechanically even timing where weight should be felt

**Personality & Acting Choices (30%)**

- At least one deliberate deviation from literal reference in service of character
- Secondary details reinforce the character's established personality
- Final animation reads as specific to this character, not generic
- Acting choices are clear and intentional, not accidental

**Reflection (15%)**

- Thoughtful, specific reflection on the process
- Clear identification of what video reference revealed
- Honest account of where/why the animation deviated from reference
- Specific goal identified for Challenge G3

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Reference isn't a crutch — it's a way of noticing details your imagination alone would skip. The real skill is knowing when to follow the reference exactly and when to deliberately deviate from it to serve your specific character.
