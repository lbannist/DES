## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Assemble a polished demo reel built around their Challenge J3 render
- Apply a stream-specific final quality pass appropriate to their competition category
- Complete a timed mock competition round under judged, rubric-based conditions

## 💭 Production Question

### If this reel and this timed round were the only things a judge ever saw from you, would they see your best work?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Reel Assembly Around Your J3 Render (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Build Your Reel**

1. Your Challenge J3 final render is your reel's centerpiece — treat it as the strongest, most technically impressive piece you have.
2. Gather 2–4 additional strong pieces from across the program (a Grade 10 walk cycle, a Grade 11 environment, a Gr12 Challenge G4 performance shot, an I4 texture pack) to show range alongside your J3 piece.
3. Trim each clip to its strongest moments, arrange them in an intentional order (your J3 piece can open or close the reel — decide which makes the stronger impression), and add clean transitions and a title card.
4. Add music/SFX with levels balanced so nothing overpowers the visuals, and export at the resolution/length required for this year's submission.

**Requirements**

- Challenge J3 render featured prominently as the reel's centerpiece
- 2–4 additional pieces included showing range across the program
- Clean transitions, title card, and balanced audio
- Export meets required resolution/length specs

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Stream-Specific Portfolio Finalization (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Apply Your Final Polish**

**Animation stream:** Re-check your featured performance shot's timing and posing one final time. Apply basic color correction across your reel for visual consistency.

**Game Art stream:** Capture a final walkthrough or turntable of your best environment/asset work (potentially your Challenge H4 level, redressed with an I4 texture pack) alongside your J3 render. Apply a final lighting polish pass.

1. Prepare a short (3–5 sentence) artist statement describing your primary portfolio piece and the specific skills it demonstrates.
2. If submitting to an online portfolio platform (ArtStation, Sketchfab), format your piece and statement accordingly.

**Requirements**

- Stream-appropriate final polish pass completed
- Clear, concise artist statement prepared
- Presentation-ready for the relevant submission format

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Mock Competition Round (Days 3–5, timed conditions)

**📖 Read and Plan**

1. Your teacher will distribute a timed brief matching your stream (an animation prompt with a required character action/emotion, or a Game Art prompt with a required prop/environment/interactive theme).
2. Read the brief fully before starting, note required elements, and spend a fixed, limited planning window sketching/planning before touching the computer.
3. Set personal time checkpoints for the remaining working time based on your own experience with how long each stage takes you.

**✏️ Work the Brief Under Time Pressure**

1. Execute your plan, checking progress against your checkpoints.
2. If falling behind, make deliberate decisions about what to simplify rather than letting quality slip everywhere — prioritize what the rubric weights most heavily.
3. Reserve the final portion of your time strictly for polish and export.
4. Submit at the actual time limit, even if incomplete.

**Requirements**

- Brief requirements fully read and understood before starting
- Personal time-checkpoint plan created and genuinely followed
- Work submitted at the actual time limit

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Judged Feedback & Targeted Iteration (Days 5–6)

**📖 Receive Judging**

1. Your Checkpoint 3 piece will be judged against a rubric mirroring Skills Alberta's actual categories (technical execution, creativity, adherence to brief, time management).
2. Receive scores/feedback without immediately defending your choices — listen and take notes first, then ask clarifying questions.

**✏️ Iterate**

1. Identify the single rubric category where you scored weakest or received the most consistent critical feedback.
2. Write a specific, actionable plan for addressing it — not "improve texturing," but something concrete like "add a second wear layer targeting the grip area."
3. Apply this targeted fix to your mock competition piece (or your Checkpoint 1 reel centerpiece, whichever benefits more) and document the before/after.

**Requirements**

- Full rubric feedback received and documented
- A specific, actionable improvement plan written
- Targeted improvement clearly visible in a before/after comparison

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Program Reflection (Day 6)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Looking back at your very first Grade 10 walk cycle, what's the single biggest change in how you approach a project now?
2. Which specific skill from this entire 3-year program do you feel most confident in? Which do you still want to keep developing?
3. What did the mock competition process teach you that regular class assignments didn't?
4. What advice would you give a Grade 10 student just starting this program?
