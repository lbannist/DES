# 3D Animation 12 – Storyboard-to-Final Performance Shot

## Unit 1: Advanced Character Animation — Challenge G4 (Unit Capstone)

**Time:** 1.5–2 Weeks (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** *[insert due date]*

*Builds on Challenges G1–G3 and Gr11's storyboarding (Challenge C5).*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Take a storyboard/animatic through blocking to a fully polished final shot
- Apply advanced rigging, weight/acting, and secondary/overlapping action together in one piece
- Compare a finished shot back against its original storyboard to evaluate staging and intent

This is the unit capstone — every skill from Challenges G1–G3 should be visible here.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How much of your original intention survives from storyboard to final polished shot — and where did the piece get better by changing?

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Storyboard/Animatic Selection & Refresh (Day 1)

**✏️ Choose and Refine Your Plan**

1. Select a storyboard/animatic from Gr11 Challenge C5, or create a new short one (5–15 seconds) featuring a clear character action with emotional weight.
2. Review it against everything you've learned since (acting/weight from G2, overlap from G3) and make any needed adjustments to staging or timing before starting 3D work.
3. Confirm your shot list and finalize your camera plan.

**Requirements**

- A clear, finalized storyboard/animatic to work from
- Any necessary staging/timing adjustments made before blocking begins

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Blocking (Days 2–3)

**✏️ Block In Your Shot**

1. Using your Challenge G1 rig, block in rough key poses that match your storyboard's staging and timing.
2. Reference your Challenge G2 video-reference habits if the action calls for a physical performance — record new reference if needed for this specific shot.
3. Check your blocked animation against your storyboard/animatic — does the staging and pacing still work in 3D, or does something need adjusting now that it's no longer just drawings?

**Requirements**

- Full shot blocked in rough key poses
- Blocking checked against the original storyboard/animatic for staging and timing fidelity

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Refinement: Weight, Acting & Overlap (Days 3–5)

**✏️ Bring the Shot to Life**

1. Refine your blocked poses with the weight and acting principles from Challenge G2 — is the character's effort and personality clearly readable?
2. Add secondary and overlapping action (Challenge G3) to any relevant elements — hair, clothing, props.
3. Use your advanced rigging tools from Challenge G1 (IK/FK switching, corrective shape keys) wherever the shot's specific poses demand them.
4. Play back the full shot repeatedly, checking for any remaining stiff, mechanical, or unconvincing moments.

**Requirements**

- Clear evidence of weight and acting principles applied throughout
- At least one instance of secondary/overlapping action present
- Advanced rigging tools used where the shot's poses required them

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Polish & Final Render (Days 5–6)

**✏️ Final Polish Pass**

1. Do a final full playback and clean up any remaining rough spots in timing or posing.
2. Apply lighting and rendering settings appropriate to the shot's mood, using your Gr11 Unit 2 knowledge.
3. Render the final shot at full quality.
4. Compare your finished shot side by side with your original Checkpoint 1 storyboard/animatic and note what changed and why.

**Requirements**

- Fully polished final animation with no unaddressed rough spots
- Appropriate lighting/rendering applied
- A clear written comparison between the original plan and the final result

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Peer Critique

**✏️ Peer Critique**

1. Share your finished shot with a peer group.
2. Ask specifically: does the acting/performance read clearly? Does anything feel stiff or unconvincing?
3. Note at least two specific pieces of feedback and how you'll address them before final submission.

---

## Checkpoint 6 — Reflection

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What changed between your original storyboard and your final shot, and why did it get better (or did it)?
2. Which single skill from Challenges G1–G3 do you think improved this shot the most?
3. What was the hardest part of taking this piece from blocking to full polish?
4. How ready do you feel for Unreal-based work in Unit 2, based on this finished character shot?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Staging & Storyboard Fidelity (15%)**

- Final shot's staging clearly traces back to the original storyboard/animatic
- Deliberate changes from the original plan are justified and improve the piece
- Camera work supports the story clearly

**Rigging & Technical Execution (20%)**

- Advanced rigging tools (IK/FK switching, corrective shape keys) used appropriately
- No unresolved deformation problems in the final shot
- Rig fully supports the performance without visible technical distraction

**Acting, Weight & Personality (25%)**

- Character's effort and weight are clearly readable
- Performance reflects the character's established personality
- Reference-informed choices are evident in the final polish

**Secondary & Overlapping Action (20%)**

- At least one clear instance of overlapping action present
- Overlap is well-timed and complements the main performance
- Detail adds genuine believability rather than distraction

**Critique Response (5%)**

- Evidence peer feedback was genuinely considered and applied
- At least two specific pieces of feedback documented and addressed

**Reflection (15%)**

- Thoughtful, specific reflection on the whole unit
- Honest comparison between the original plan and final result
- Clear identification of the most impactful skill applied
- Realistic self-assessment of readiness for Unit 2

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

This is where an entire unit's worth of technical rigging skill, acting judgment, and secondary detail come together in one finished shot — the exact combination that separates a technically competent animation from one that actually performs, which is what Skills Alberta judges are looking for.
