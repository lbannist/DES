# Animation 30 – Sound Design & Timing Integration

## Unit J: Voice & Integration — Challenge J1

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Score an original animation with sound effects and/or music
- Time keyframes to land precisely on specific beats or sound cues
- Read an audio waveform to inform animation timing
- Compare the same animation with two different soundtracks to observe timing shifts

Students will learn that sound isn't added after animation is finished — it's a timing tool that shapes the animation itself.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Does sound just accompany your animation, or does it drive the timing?

Building on the G10 Krita audio-import step, this assignment treats sound as a precision timing tool, not background ambience.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Sound & Timing Study**

Watch 2–3 clips where animation timing is clearly synced to specific sound cues (a hit landing exactly on a musical beat, a footstep matched to a sound effect). Answer:

1. Where exactly does the key animation pose land relative to the sound cue?
2. What would change if the timing were off by even a few frames?
3. How does the waveform (if visible) relate to the visual motion?
4. Where is sound used to imply something happening off-screen?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Choose Your Action and Soundtrack**

Select a short action (2–5 seconds) and two different soundtracks or sound effect sets to score it with.

**Plan**

- Import both soundtracks into Krita and note the waveform's key hit points/beats
- Sketch your key poses and mark which pose should land on which sound cue
- Predict, in writing, how the two different soundtracks might change your timing

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Animate the same action twice — once scored to each soundtrack — using your plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- Two versions of the same base action (Version A and Version B), each 2–5 seconds
- Keyframes clearly timed to hit points in each respective soundtrack
- At least one moment in each version where a pose lands exactly on a sound cue
- Audio imported and synced within the Krita timeline

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. How did your timing differ between Version A and Version B?
2. Which soundtrack made the animation feel stronger, and why?
3. Did reading the waveform change any poses you had already planned?
4. What did you learn about timing that you'll carry into future projects?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Sound-to-Timing Sync (40%)**

- Key poses land precisely on sound cues/hit points
- Clear evidence of waveform-informed timing decisions

**Technical Execution (30%)**

- Smooth animation independent of sound
- Clean audio import and export

**Comparative Craft (20%)**

- Meaningful, identifiable difference in timing between Version A and B

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful comparison of the two soundtracks' effect on timing

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Sound doesn't just support animation — it can dictate its rhythm. The best-timed animation often starts with the animator's ear, not just their eye.
