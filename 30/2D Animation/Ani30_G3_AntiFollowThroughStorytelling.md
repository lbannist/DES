# Animation 30 – Anticipation & Follow-Through as Storytelling

## Unit G: Performance Fundamentals — Challenge G3 (Unit Capstone)

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Apply anticipation and follow-through across a full action, not an isolated pose
- Use anticipation to build audience expectation and misdirect before a payoff
- Justify each animation principle choice in terms of character intent
- Combine skills from G1 (object/weight) and G2 (reference/exaggeration) into one performance

Students will learn that anticipation and follow-through are storytelling tools, not just technical requirements.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How do you make an audience feel something is about to happen — before it does?

This is the Unit G capstone. Bring forward your object-animation weight sense (G1) and your reference-based exaggeration skills (G2) into one performance built around a deliberate "wind-up."

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Anticipation Study**

Watch 2–3 professional clips where anticipation clearly builds expectation before an action (a wind-up before a punch, a crouch before a jump, a pause before a scream). Answer:

1. How long is the anticipation held relative to the action itself?
2. What does the anticipation pose "promise" the audience?
3. Does the payoff match, exceed, or subvert that promise?
4. Where do you see follow-through after the main action completes?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Plan Your Action**

Choose a short action with a clear "wind-up" and payoff — the payoff can match the anticipation OR intentionally misdirect (e.g. character winds up to throw something hard, but gently tosses it instead).

**Plan**

- Sketch your key poses: anticipation, main action, follow-through/settle
- Note which principle each pose is demonstrating and why it belongs there
- Decide whether you're using a character, creature, or object (you may reuse your G1 object or G2 character)

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your animation in Krita, using your plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 4–7 second animation
- Clear anticipation pose held long enough to register with the audience
- Main action that either pays off or intentionally subverts the anticipation
- Follow-through/overlapping action after the main movement completes (nothing should stop instantly)
- At least one principle from G1 or G2 (weight, exaggeration) visibly reused

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Did your payoff match or subvert the anticipation? Why did you choose that?
2. How long did you hold your anticipation pose, and how did you decide on that timing?
3. What would happen to your animation if you removed the follow-through entirely?
4. How did skills from G1 (object animation) or G2 (reference performance) show up in this piece?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Technical Animation (35%)**

- Clear, well-timed anticipation pose
- Visible follow-through/overlapping action
- Smooth, natural spacing throughout

**Storytelling Intent (35%)**

- Anticipation builds genuine audience expectation
- Payoff is a deliberate creative choice (match or subversion), not accidental
- Principle choices are clearly justified by character intent

**Integration (20%)**

- Visible carry-over of skills from G1 and/or G2

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful justification of timing and payoff choices

## 💡 Key Lesson

Anticipation is a promise to your audience. Follow-through is keeping your word after the action ends. Together, they're what separates motion from performance.
