# Animation 30 – Prop & Object Animation

## Unit G: Performance Fundamentals — Challenge G1

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Animate an inanimate object so it reads as "alive" without a face or character design
- Rely entirely on timing, arcs, and squash & stretch to communicate personality
- Apply weight and material properties to motion (a brick moves differently than a balloon)
- Practice staging so a single object reads clearly against a simple background

Students will learn that personality and emotion can be communicated through physics-driven motion alone — no face required.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Can motion alone make an audience believe something is alive?

This assignment revisits the G10 bouncing ball exercise, but pushes it further: instead of a generic ball, students choose a specific object with real-world weight and material, and must make it "perform" using only motion.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Object Animation Study**

Watch 2–3 examples of object animation (e.g. Pixar shorts featuring inanimate objects, classic animated household items). While watching, answer:

1. What single action tells you the object is "alive"?
2. How does timing communicate the object's weight?
3. Where do you see squash & stretch used on a rigid object?
4. What emotion is the object expressing, and how do you know?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Choose Your Object**

Pick one object with a clear material and weight (e.g. a heavy stone, a light paper bag, a bouncy rubber ball, a stiff wooden chair).

**Plan**

- Sketch your object in 3–5 key poses that show a simple action (waking up, escaping, reacting to something)
- Note the material properties that will affect its motion (rigid vs. squishy, heavy vs. light)
- Identify where squash & stretch, arcs, and timing will each appear in your action

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your object animation in Krita, using your plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 5–8 second animation
- No face, eyes, or added character features — personality must come from motion only
- Clear demonstration of weight through timing and spacing
- At least one moment of squash & stretch appropriate to the object's material
- Motion path should show a visible arc, not a straight line

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What single motion choice did the most work in making your object feel alive?
2. How did your object's material affect your timing decisions?
3. What was hardest to communicate without a face?
4. If you added a face, what do you think would change — for better or worse?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Technical Animation (40%)**

- Clear weight communicated through timing
- Squash & stretch appropriate to material
- Visible arcs in motion path
- Clean, readable staging

**Performance & Personality (30%)**

- Object reads as "alive" without added features
- Motion communicates a clear emotion or intent
- Action is simple and well-chosen for the format

**Visual Presentation (20%)**

- Clear silhouette against background
- Consistent object design across frames
- Professional export quality

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful analysis of motion choices
- Understanding of weight/material relationship

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Personality doesn't come from a face — it comes from how something moves. Professional animators use timing, weight, and arcs to bring even the simplest object to life.
