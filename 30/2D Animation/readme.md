# Group G: Performance Fundamentals

Goal: deepen acting and physical believability before adding story/camera complexity

#1 — Anticipation & Follow-Through as Storytelling
#9 — Prop & Object Animation (Non-Character Performance)
#8 — Adapting a Reference Performance

These all live at the level of a single action or performance. Object animation strips away the face to force pure physics-based acting, the reference-performance project reintroduces a human source to exaggerate from, and anticipation/follow-through ties both together as the underlying principle work. A logical closing challenge: animate a reference-based performance that leads with a strong anticipation beat.

# Group H: Story & Camera Literacy

Goal: move from single actions to planned sequences, told visually

#6 — Storyboarding as a Standalone Craft
#11 — Analyzing and Reverse-Engineering Professional Work
#3 — Cinematic Camera Work (Beyond Parallax)

Storyboarding teaches sequencing and staging on paper; reverse-engineering a professional clip teaches students to read those same choices in finished work; camera work lets them apply both to their own shot. A natural end point: storyboard a short sequence, then animate one shot from it with a deliberate camera move.

# Group I: World & Mood Building

Goal: shift focus from character to environment as a storytelling tool

#7 — Environmental Storytelling & World-Building
#10 — Color Theory & Mood Through Animation
#5 — Cross-Technique Fusion (2D + Effects/Compositing)

World-building establishes a living environment, color theory teaches how to shape its emotional tone, and effects/compositing adds the final layer of atmosphere and integration. A strong closer: take the environment from the first project and re-treat it in two color/mood variants, then add one integrated environmental effect.
