# Animation 30 – Term Outline

## From Fundamentals to Voice: A Four-Unit Progression

**Time:** Full Term (4 Units)

---

## 🎯 Big Picture

Grade 10 built technical fundamentals (Krita, the 12 principles, bouncing ball, walk cycles). Grade 11 introduced production thinking (reusable assets, parallax, tweening, lighting/effects). Grade 12 moves students from *executing techniques* to *making deliberate creative choices* — culminating in a capstone that pulls performance, story, world, and personal voice together into one polished piece.

Each unit below builds on the last. Skills are not retired after their unit — they carry forward and get folded into later, more complex challenges.

---

## Unit G — Performance Fundamentals

> 🎯 **Focus:** Deepen acting and physical believability before adding story or camera complexity.

**Why this order:** Before students can tell a story or compose a shot, they need to be fluent in *making something feel alive*. This unit strips away story and camera so all attention goes to motion, weight, and intent.

**Challenge G1 — Prop & Object Animation**
Bring an inanimate object to life using only physics-driven principles — no face, no character design. Forces reliance on timing, arcs, and squash & stretch to communicate personality without a face. Compare to the G10 bouncing ball: same physics, now applied with weight, material, and intent.

**Challenge G2 — Adapting a Reference Performance**
Film a short real-world action (self or volunteer), then animate a stylized version of it. Builds on the "find your own reference clip" habit from earlier grades, but now with original, self-shot reference. The goal is exaggeration from real reference, not frame-for-frame copying.

**Challenge G3 — Anticipation & Follow-Through as Storytelling**
Animate an action with a clear "wind-up" that misdirects the audience before the payoff. Revisits G10 principles (squash/stretch, timing) but requires anticipation and follow-through to serve character intent, not just technical correctness.

**⭐ Unit Capstone:** Animate a reference-based performance (G2) that leads with a strong anticipation beat (G3), applied to a non-human object or prop (G1) where possible.

---

## Unit H — Story & Camera Literacy

> 🎯 **Focus:** Move from single actions to planned sequences, told visually.

**Why this order:** With performance skills established, students learn to sequence and frame those performances — reading professional choices before making their own.

**Challenge H1 — Storyboarding as a Standalone Craft**
Storyboard a 60-second scene from an existing script or song — no animation required. Teaches shot-to-shot flow, continuity of screen direction, and staging at the sequence level, not just the single-shot level.

**Challenge H2 — Analyzing and Reverse-Engineering Professional Work**
Break down a 10–15 second professional clip frame-by-frame, identifying every one of the 12 principles present, plus staging, camera, and timing choices. Recreate the clip's key poses and timing to internalize professional pacing.

**Challenge H3 — Cinematic Camera Work (Beyond Parallax)**
Extend the G11 parallax/panning assignment with dolly, zoom, rack focus, and shake. Requires a storyboard before animating, with every camera choice justified.

**⭐ Unit Capstone:** Storyboard a short sequence (H1), then animate one shot from it (H3) using a technique or timing choice identified during the reverse-engineering exercise (H2).

---

## Unit I — World & Mood Building

> 🎯 **Focus:** Shift focus from character to environment as a storytelling tool.

**Why this order:** Now that students can perform and frame an action, they learn to build the *space* that action lives in — and to shape how that space feels.

**Challenge I1 — Environmental Storytelling & World-Building**
Design and animate an environment (abandoned room, bustling market, quiet forest) that tells a story through detail and subtle motion alone — no characters. Extends the G11 parallax/lighting assignment into full scene design.

**Challenge I2 — Color Theory & Mood Through Animation**
Animate the same short scene with two different color palettes (e.g. warm/inviting vs. cold/tense) and compare audience reaction. Builds on G11 lighting/effects skills, now applied deliberately for emotional effect.

**Challenge I3 — Cross-Technique Fusion (2D + Effects/Compositing)**
Animate a character reacting to an environmental effect (rain, wind, fire nearby) so the two systems interact convincingly. Introduces basic compositing — layering character over background, unifying lighting and color.

**⭐ Unit Capstone:** Take the environment built in I1, re-treat it in two color/mood variants (I2), then add one integrated environmental effect (I3).

---

## Unit J — Voice & Integration (Term Capstone)

> 🎯 **Focus:** Bring performance, story, and world together into a personal, technically ambitious final piece.

**Why this order:** This unit is designed to draw on nearly everything from Units A–C: performance from A, camera and sequencing from B, mood and effects from C — while adding two final, professional-level layers: sound and personal style.

**Challenge J1 — Sound Design & Timing Integration**
Score an animation with sound effects and/or music, timing keyframes to land on specific beats or cues. Builds on the G10 Krita audio-import step, now used for precision timing rather than just ambience.

**Challenge J2 — Personal Style Development**
Study 3–4 animators/studios with distinct visual styles, then render a stock action in someone else's style before reworking it in the student's own emerging style. Tracked through a term-long journal of recurring visual choices.

**Challenge J3 — Compositing Animation into Live-Action Camera Footage**
Film a real environment with intentional camera movement (hallway walk, desk pan, outdoor tracking shot), then animate a character and/or props into that footage — matching camera motion, perspective, and lighting throughout. The most technically demanding challenge of the term: true match-move/compositing work.

**⭐ Term Capstone:** A short animated piece — performed (A), sequenced and shot (B), set in a built world with mood (C) — composited into live-action footage (J3), scored (J1), and rendered in the student's own developing style (J2).

---

## 📊 Suggested Term Flow

| Unit | Focus | Weeks (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| A — Performance Fundamentals | Physical believability, no story/camera | 1–3 |
| B — Story & Camera Literacy | Sequencing, framing, professional analysis | 4–6 |
| C — World & Mood Building | Environment, color, atmosphere | 7–9 |
| D — Voice & Integration | Sound, style, live-action compositing | 10–13 |

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

By Grade 12, the question is no longer *"can you execute the technique?"* — it's *"what are you choosing to say with it?"* Every unit capstone is a chance to make a deliberate creative decision, not just complete a checklist.
