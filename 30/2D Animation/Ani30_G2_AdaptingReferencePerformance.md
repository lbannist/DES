# Animation 30 – Adapting a Reference Performance

## Unit G: Performance Fundamentals — Challenge G2

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Film original reference footage of a short physical action
- Translate real human performance into stylized character animation
- Practice exaggeration from real reference rather than copying it frame-for-frame
- Compare raw reference to final animation to evaluate their own creative choices

Students will learn that reference footage is a starting point for exaggeration, not a template to trace.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How much do you keep from real life, and how much do you push?

Earlier grades used found reference clips. This assignment requires students to shoot their own reference — putting them in control of the performance from the very first step.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Reference Study**

Watch a professional animated scene alongside its original live-action reference footage (many studios release these side-by-side). Answer:

1. What did the animators keep exactly as filmed?
2. What did they exaggerate, and why?
3. What did they cut or simplify?
4. How did timing change between the reference and the final animation?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning and Filming (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Film Your Reference**

Choose a short physical action (2–5 seconds) — a reaction, a small gesture, a simple movement. Film yourself or a volunteer performing it several times.

**Plan**

- Select your best take and note the key poses within it (timestamp them)
- Sketch thumbnails of the 3–5 poses you'll build your animation around
- Identify one thing you will exaggerate beyond what the reference shows

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your animation in Krita, using your reference footage and thumbnails as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 3–6 second animation
- Character/creature design of your choice (does not need to match your own appearance)
- Reference footage must be visibly informing the key poses (include reference stills in your submission)
- At least one clearly exaggerated moment beyond what the reference shows

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did you keep directly from your reference footage?
2. What did you intentionally push further, and why?
3. Where did the reference help you, and where did it limit you?
4. Would you shoot your reference differently next time? How?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Technical Animation (35%)**

- Poses clearly informed by reference footage
- Smooth, natural timing
- Consistent character construction

**Performance & Exaggeration (35%)**

- Clear evidence of creative exaggeration beyond raw reference
- Believable weight and physicality
- Personality comes through in the performance

**Visual Presentation (20%)**

- Clean staging and readable silhouette
- Professional export quality

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful comparison between reference and final animation

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Reference footage tells you what's true. Animation lets you decide what's interesting. The best animators know exactly where one ends and the other begins.
