# Animation 30 – Environmental Storytelling & World-Building

## Unit I: World & Mood Building — Challenge I1

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Design and animate an environment that tells a story with no characters present
- Communicate mood and narrative through detail, composition, and subtle motion
- Extend parallax and layered depth skills from Grade 11 into full scene design
- Add at least one animated "life" element to avoid a static-feeling scene

Students will learn that a well-designed environment can imply an entire story before a single character appears.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What can an empty room tell you about who lives there?

No characters are allowed in this assignment. The environment itself must do all the storytelling.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Environmental Storytelling Study**

Study 2–3 examples of environments that clearly imply a story or character without anyone present (an abandoned room, a messy desk, a quiet forest clearing with signs of recent activity). Answer:

1. What specific details suggest a story or a person who was recently there?
2. How does the composition guide your eye to the most important details first?
3. What role does lighting play in the mood?
4. What subtle motion (if any) keeps the scene from feeling frozen?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Choose Your Environment**

Pick a setting that implies a story: an abandoned room, a bustling market, a quiet forest, or an approved original concept.

**Plan**

- Sketch your environment with foreground, midground, and background layers
- List 3–5 specific details that imply story or character (without showing anyone)
- Decide on your one animated "life" element (dust motes, flickering sign, distant movement, swaying branch, etc.)

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your environment in Krita, using your plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 8–12 second animation (looping or single continuous shot)
- No characters present
- Layered depth (foreground/midground/background)
- Minimum 3 specific storytelling details visible in the scene
- At least 1 animated "life" element

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What story do you think a viewer would infer from your scene, without you explaining it?
2. Which detail are you proudest of, and why?
3. How did your "life" element change the feeling of the scene compared to a fully static version?
4. What would you add if you had another full day to work on this?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Environmental Storytelling (40%)**

- Scene clearly implies a story or character without anyone present
- Details are specific and purposeful, not generic clutter

**Technical Execution (30%)**

- Effective use of layered depth
- Animated "life" element is smooth and well-integrated
- Consistent lighting and perspective

**Composition (20%)**

- Eye is guided to the most important details
- Clean, readable staging

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful analysis of storytelling choices

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A scene doesn't need a character to have a story. Every detail an animator chooses to include — or leave out — is a storytelling decision.
