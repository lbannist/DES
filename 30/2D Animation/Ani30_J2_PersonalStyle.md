# Animation 30 – Personal Style Development

## Unit J: Voice & Integration — Challenge J2

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods), with a term-long journal component
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Identify what makes 3–4 professional animators/studios visually recognizable
- Practice working in someone else's style before returning to their own
- Track recurring personal choices (line weight, proportions, color) across the term
- Frame a finished piece explicitly as "their style," not just a technical exercise

Students will learn that personal style isn't something that appears automatically — it's something they can notice, name, and develop deliberately.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What makes your work recognizably yours?

This assignment has a term-long component: students should be filling out their style journal throughout Unit J, not just in the final week.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Style Study**

Choose 3–4 animators or studios with clearly distinct visual styles. For each, answer:

1. What specific visual choices make this style recognizable (line weight, proportions, color, shapes)?
2. How does the style affect the mood or feeling of the work?
3. What's one choice you could imagine borrowing, and one you couldn't imagine using?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Style Study Exercise (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Redraw in Another Style**

Choose one stock character or simple action and redraw/animate it in the style of one of the animators/studios you studied in Checkpoint 1.

**Requirements**

- 2–4 second animation or a sequence of 3–5 stylized poses
- Deliberately match line weight, proportion, and color choices from the studied style
- Short written note identifying which specific choices you borrowed

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Personal Style Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Reverse the Exercise**

Take the same character or action from Checkpoint 2 and rework it in your own emerging style.

**Requirements**

- Same length/format as Checkpoint 2
- Should feel like a deliberate departure from the studied style, not an accident
- Review your term-long style journal entries and note at least 3 recurring choices you're incorporating

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What specific choices define your emerging personal style, based on your journal entries?
2. What did studying another style teach you about your own?
3. Which of your recurring choices do you want to keep developing, and which might you drop?
4. How would you describe your style to someone who's never seen your work?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Style Study Accuracy (25%)**

- Clear, deliberate matching of the studied animator/studio's visual choices

**Personal Style Development (35%)**

- Clear, identifiable personal choices distinct from the studied style
- Evidence of consistency with the term-long style journal

**Technical Execution (20%)**

- Clean, professional animation quality in both pieces

**Reflection & Journal (20%)**

- Thoughtful, specific articulation of personal style
- Evidence of ongoing tracking throughout the term, not just a single entry

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Style isn't a filter you apply at the end — it's the sum of small, consistent choices you make every time you draw. Naming those choices is the first step to owning them.
