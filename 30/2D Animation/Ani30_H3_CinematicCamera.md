# Animation 30 – Cinematic Camera Work (Beyond Parallax)

## Unit H: Story & Camera Literacy — Challenge H3 (Unit Capstone)

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Apply camera moves — dolly, zoom, rack focus, shake — to an animated scene
- Use shot composition (rule of thirds, framing for emotion) deliberately
- Justify every camera choice in a storyboard before animating
- Combine skills from H1 (storyboarding) and H2 (professional pacing) into one shot

Students will learn that the camera is not neutral — it actively shapes what an audience feels.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### How does the camera change what the audience feels, not just what they see?

This is the Unit H capstone. Take a shot from the storyboard you built in H1, apply the pacing awareness from H2, and animate it with deliberate camera work.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Camera Study**

Watch 2–3 clips that use dolly, zoom, rack focus, or shake with clear intent. Answer:

1. What emotion did each camera move create or reinforce?
2. Where would the shot have felt different with a static camera instead?
3. How fast or slow was the camera move, and how did that pacing affect the feeling?
4. Which composition choices (rule of thirds, framing) supported the camera move?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Choose Your Shot**

Select one shot from your H1 storyboard (or a new short scene, instructor-approved) to animate in full.

**Plan**

- Storyboard the shot specifically, marking the camera's start and end framing
- Identify which camera move(s) you'll use and why they suit the story moment
- Note your composition plan — where the subject sits in frame at key points

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Build your shot in Krita, using your plan from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- 5–10 second animated shot
- At least one deliberate camera move (dolly, zoom, rack focus, or shake)
- Composition follows the rule of thirds or another intentional framing choice at key moments
- Camera timing paced deliberately (not simply linear/constant speed throughout)

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What did your camera move add that a static shot couldn't?
2. How did you decide on the pacing of your camera move?
3. Which composition choice are you proudest of, and why?
4. How did your H1 storyboard and H2 pacing analysis inform this shot?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Camera Work (40%)**

- Deliberate, well-executed camera move
- Camera pacing supports the emotional intent of the shot

**Composition (30%)**

- Intentional framing throughout (rule of thirds or equivalent)
- Subject stays readable as the camera moves

**Integration (20%)**

- Clear connection to H1 storyboard planning and H2 pacing analysis

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful justification of camera and composition choices

## ⭐ Unit H Capstone Note

This assignment closes out Unit H. You've now planned a sequence on paper (H1), studied how professionals pace and stage action (H2), and applied deliberate camera work to your own shot (H3). These sequencing and framing skills carry directly into Unit I, where you'll build entire environments the camera can move through.

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

A static camera shows an audience a scene. A moving camera tells them how to feel about it. Every camera choice is a storytelling choice — never just a technical one.
