# Animation 30 – Storyboarding as a Standalone Craft

## Unit H: Story & Camera Literacy — Challenge H1

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Storyboard a full 60-second scene without producing any final animation
- Communicate action, staging, and continuity of screen direction through thumbnails alone
- Practice sequencing shots so a story reads clearly from panel to panel
- Interpret an existing script or song into a visual sequence

Students will learn that storyboarding is its own discipline — planning that lives entirely on paper before a single frame is animated.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### Can your story be understood before anyone animates a single frame?

No animation is produced in this assignment. The entire grade is based on whether the sequence of drawings communicates the scene clearly on their own.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Storyboard Study**

Study 2–3 professional storyboards (many productions release theirs publicly) alongside their finished scenes. Answer:

1. How much detail is in each panel — is it rough or highly finished?
2. How do arrows or annotations communicate camera movement or character motion?
3. How does screen direction stay consistent from panel to panel?
4. Where does the board show more than one panel for a single continuous action, and why?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Planning (Day 1–2)

**✏️ Choose Your Source**

Select a short script excerpt or song (30–60 seconds) provided by your instructor, or an original scene you approve with your instructor first.

**Plan**

- Break the scene into shots — list each shot with a one-line description of what happens
- Identify where screen direction (left-to-right, right-to-left) needs to stay consistent
- Note where a close-up, wide shot, or camera move would best serve the story

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Storyboard Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Draw your full storyboard, using your shot list from Checkpoint 2 as your guide.**

**Requirements**

- Minimum 12 panels covering the full 60-second scene
- Each panel includes a simple frame/camera indication (wide, medium, close-up)
- Arrows or annotations showing character or camera movement where relevant
- Consistent screen direction maintained across the sequence
- Panels are numbered and match your shot list

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Which panel was hardest to stage clearly, and why?
2. Where did you break the scene into more panels than expected, and why?
3. Did you maintain consistent screen direction throughout? Where was it hardest?
4. If you were to animate this board, which shot would take the longest to produce?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Sequencing & Staging (40%)**

- Story is understandable from panels alone
- Clear shot variety (wide/medium/close-up) used purposefully
- Consistent screen direction throughout

**Communication of Motion (30%)**

- Arrows/annotations clearly show movement and camera intent
- Panel count appropriately breaks down complex actions

**Craft & Organization (20%)**

- Panels are numbered, legible, and match the shot list
- Clean, professional presentation

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful analysis of staging decisions

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

If a storyboard can't communicate the story, no amount of polished animation will fix it. Professional productions catch story problems on paper — where they're cheap to fix — not in final frames.
