# Animation 30 – Analyzing and Reverse-Engineering Professional Work

## Unit H: Story & Camera Literacy — Challenge H2

**Time:** 1 Week (4–5 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** Following Monday before class

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Break down a professional animated clip frame-by-frame
- Identify all 12 principles of animation present in a finished piece
- Recreate key poses and timing to internalize professional pacing
- Compare their own "before" and "after" attempts at a similar action

Students will learn to read professional work analytically, not just watch it passively.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What can you learn from a professional clip that you can't learn from a tutorial?

This assignment has two halves: analysis (breaking down someone else's work) and application (proving what you learned by recreating it).

---

## Checkpoint 1 — "Before" Attempt (Day 1)

**✏️ Baseline Animation**

Before analyzing any reference, animate a short action of your choosing (2–4 seconds) using only your current skills. This is your baseline — do not revise it later.

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Research and Analysis (Day 1–2)

**📖 Clip Breakdown**

Select a 10–15 second professional clip (instructor-approved) featuring an action similar in spirit to your Checkpoint 1 baseline.

**✏️ Frame-by-Frame Analysis**

For your chosen clip, document:

1. Every one of the 12 principles you can identify, with the timestamp where each occurs
2. The staging choices (where the action is placed in frame, what draws the eye)
3. Camera behavior, if any (static, panning, cutting)
4. The overall timing — count roughly how many key poses occur and how they're spaced

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Recreation Production (Days 2–4)

**✏️ Recreate the clip's key poses and timing as closely as possible.**

This is a study, not a trace — redraw the poses in your own hand rather than tracing frames directly.

**Document Settings**

- 1920 × 1080
- 12 FPS

**Requirements**

- Recreate 4–6 key poses from the reference clip, matching their timing/spacing
- Include a brief written note under each pose identifying which principle it demonstrates
- Animation length should roughly match the reference clip's pacing

---

## Checkpoint 4 — "After" Attempt and Reflection (Day 5)

**✏️ Second Attempt**

Re-animate the same action you did in Checkpoint 1 (your baseline), now applying what you learned from the professional clip.

**✏️ Answer:**

1. Show your "before" and "after" side by side — what specifically changed?
2. Which principle from the professional clip made the biggest difference?
3. What surprised you about the professional clip's timing once you counted the poses?
4. What will you carry forward from this analysis into future assignments?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Analysis Quality (35%)**

- Accurate identification of principles with correct timestamps
- Clear understanding of staging and camera choices
- Accurate pose/timing count

**Recreation Accuracy (30%)**

- Key poses and timing closely match the reference clip
- Poses are redrawn, not traced, in the student's own hand

**Growth Between Before/After (25%)**

- Clear, specific improvement from baseline to final attempt
- Improvement is directly traceable to something identified in the analysis

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful, specific comparison of before/after work

---

## 💡 Key Lesson

Professional animators still study other professionals' work. Reverse-engineering isn't copying — it's the fastest way to internalize pacing and choices you can't fully see just by watching.
