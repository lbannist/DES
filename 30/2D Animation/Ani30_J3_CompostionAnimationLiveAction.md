# Animation 30 – Compositing Animation into Live-Action Camera Footage

## Unit J: Voice & Integration — Challenge J3 (Term Capstone)

**Time:** 2 Weeks (8–10 Class Periods)
**Due Date:** End of term, before final review

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this assignment students will:

- Film a real environment with intentional camera movement
- Animate a character and/or props into that footage, matching motion, perspective, and lighting
- Account for perspective and scale change as the camera moves closer to or farther from the animated element
- Combine performance (Unit G), camera/sequencing (Unit H), and world/mood (Unit I) skills with sound (J1) and personal style (J2) into one final piece

Students will learn match-move and compositing fundamentals — one of the most technically demanding and professionally relevant skills in 2D animation.

---

## 💭 Production Question

### What makes an animated element feel like it's really in the space, instead of pasted on top of it?

This is the term capstone. Every skill from Units G to J should be visible somewhere in this final piece.

---

## Checkpoint 1 — Research and Analysis (Day 1)

**📖 Match-Move & Compositing Study**

Watch 2–3 examples of 2D animation composited into live-action footage. Answer:

1. How does the animated element's perspective change as the camera moves?
2. What lighting/color choices help the animated element feel like it belongs in the shot?
3. Where do you see the animated element interact with the real environment (shadows, occlusion behind real objects)?
4. What would break the illusion if it were done poorly?

---

## Checkpoint 2 — Filming and Planning (Days 1–2)

**✏️ Film Your Environment**

Film a real environment with intentional, steady camera movement (walking down a hallway, panning across a desk, tracking through an outdoor space). Keep the movement simple and consistent — smooth pans or walks work better than complex handheld moves for a first match-move project.

**Plan**

- Choose your animated element(s): a character, creature, or prop
- Storyboard where in the footage your element will appear and how it should move relative to the camera
- Note the lighting conditions in your footage (direction, color temperature) so your animated element can match
- Decide which skills from earlier units you'll intentionally bring into this piece (e.g. a camera move from H3, a mood treatment from I2, a soundtrack approach from J1)

---

## Checkpoint 3 — Animation & Compositing Production (Days 3–8)

**✏️ Animate your character/prop and composite it into your filmed footage.**

**Document Settings**

- Match your filmed footage's resolution (1920 × 1080 recommended)
- 12 FPS animation composited over live footage

**Requirements**

- 8–15 second final piece
- Animated element(s) maintain consistent perspective and scale as the camera moves
- Lighting/color on the animated element adjusted to match the live footage
- At least one moment of interaction with the real environment (shadow, occlusion, or contact)
- Final piece includes sound design (from J1 skills) and reflects the student's personal style work (from J2)

---

## Checkpoint 4 — Polish and Critique (Day 9)

**✏️ Peer Critique**

Share your in-progress piece with a peer group using the critique structure established earlier in the term. Note at least two pieces of feedback and how you plan to address them before final submission.

---

## Checkpoint 5 — Reflection (Day 10)

**✏️ Answer:**

1. What made the animated element feel like it was really in the space, versus pasted on top of it?
2. Which was harder to get right: perspective/scale matching, or lighting/color matching?
3. How did skills from Units A, B, and C show up in this final piece? Be specific.
4. Looking back at your whole Grade 12 term, what's the single biggest thing you improved at?

## 📊 Assessment Criteria

**Match-Move & Compositing (35%)**

- Consistent perspective and scale as the camera moves
- Believable lighting/color match between animated element and footage
- At least one convincing interaction with the real environment

**Animation Quality (25%)**

- Character/prop performance meets the technical standard established across Unit G
- Timing and camera awareness reflect Unit H skills

**Integration of Term Skills (25%)**

- Clear, specific use of skills from Units A, B, C, and J1/J2
- Piece reflects the student's developing personal style

**Critique Response (5%)**

- Evidence that peer feedback was genuinely considered and applied

**Reflection (10%)**

- Thoughtful, specific reflection on both this piece and overall term growth

## 💡 Key Lesson

This is where every skill from the term comes together: performance, staging, camera, world-building, mood, sound, and style — combined with the added challenge of making it live convincingly inside the real world. This is the closest students will get, in this course, to professional-level integration work.
